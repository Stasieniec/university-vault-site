L01 Overview

Flashcards

Which three fields does NLP sit at the intersection of?

Computer science, artificial intelligence, linguistics.

Which four aspects of human language does NLP aim to model algorithmically and formally?

Word formation (morphology), sentence structure (syntax), sentence meaning (semantics), document or discourse structure.

Is written language a continuous or a discrete signal?

Discrete. Spoken language is a continuous signal.

How does question answering differ from information retrieval?

IR returns ranked documents; QA returns the actual answer.

Which types of entity does named entity recognition identify?

Persons, organizations, dates, locations.

Which NLP task estimates the attitude expressed in reviews, either positive/negative or fine-grained?

Sentiment analysis.

What was machine learning used for in pre-deep-learning NLP?

To weigh the importance of individual features (POS tags, parse trees, named entities) for prediction.

How does deep-learning NLP differ from pre-deep-learning NLP in feature engineering?

Pre-deep-learning NLP relied on engineered features; deep learning needs very little or none.

What are the disadvantages of deep learning for NLP?

Needs large amounts of training data, errors are hard to trace, can fail spectacularly.

Why do deep-learning insights transfer between NLP, vision, speech and other fields?

Neural network types are uniform and there are few application-specific features.

What is the difference between multilingual and crosslingual NLP?

Multilingual builds systems for multiple languages; crosslingual transfers information across languages.

Which scenarios count as multilingual NLP?

NER for multiple languages, language-independent parsing, cross-language classification.

Which scenarios count as crosslingual NLP?

Machine translation, crosslingual QA, crosslingual unlearning, crosslingual reasoning.

Why are LLMs English-centric?

They are trained predominantly on Internet resources.

On which languages do general LLMs such as Llama 3.1 and DeepSeek perform better?

High-resource languages.

How do dedicated multilingual models such as Aya-Expanse compare with general LLMs like Llama 3.1?

They tend to lag behind.

In which tasks can LLMs substitute or supplement humans?

Data annotation, evaluation (LLM-as-judge), dialog.

What weakness of LLMs does Monz's Arabic-to-English translation comparison show?

Fragility on non-English text: Mistral Small 3.2 hallucinated the embassy’s location.

True or false: Deep-learning NLP still requires hand-engineered features such as POS tags and parse trees.

False. Deep learning needs very little or no feature engineering; those features belong to pre-deep-learning NLP.

True or false: In pre-deep-learning NLP, different applications used different methodologies.

True. Each application had its own methodology.

True or false: A limited repertoire of neural network types applies to most NLP tasks.

True. That is one of the listed advantages of deep learning.

True or false: Dedicated multilingual models such as Aya-Expanse outperform general LLMs such as Llama 3.1 and DeepSeek.

False. Dedicated multilingual models tend to lag behind.

True or false: Crosslingual QA extracts information from resources in other languages.

True. It is a crosslingual scenario, like machine translation and crosslingual reasoning.

True or false: Because they are trained on Internet resources, LLMs are equally robust across languages.

False. Internet training data makes them English-centric and fragile on non-English text.

Multiple selection: which are true of multilingual and crosslingual NLP? (A) Machine translation is a crosslingual scenario (B) Language-independent parsing is a crosslingual scenario (C) Crosslingual reasoning combines information across languages (D) Multilingual NLP means transferring knowledge across languages

A and C. B is false: parsing is a multilingual scenario. D is false: that defines crosslingual; multilingual means building systems for multiple languages.

Multiple selection: which are true of deep learning in NLP? (A) It needs very little or no feature engineering (B) It works well with small amounts of training data (C) Its errors are easy to trace (D) Insights transfer to vision and speech because network types are uniform

A and D. B is false: it needs large amounts of training data. C is false: errors are hard to trace.

L02 Multilinguality and Writing Systems

Flashcards

According to Monz, what determines how well a language is covered by NLP resources?

Commercial interest and military relevance.

Is speaker count a good predictor of how well a language is resourced for NLP?

No. Dutch, with about 24 million speakers in a rich country, is only medium resource; money predicts better.

Why do claims that an NLP method is "language independent" deserve suspicion?

They are usually untested outside English or a very small set of languages: nobody checked.

How does Chinese mark past tense in 我不知道她去哪儿了 ("I don't know where she went")?

With the sentence-final particle 了; the verb 去 itself is unchanged.

What does case syncretism, as in Russian knígi (gen.sg, nom.pl and acc.pl), mean for NLP?

Fewer types to learn, but the surface form is ambiguous: case cannot be read off the string.

What does it mean for a language such as Finnish to be agglutinative?

Morphemes stack, each adding one piece of meaning, so words grow without bound.

Why can more training data not fix a fixed word-level vocabulary for Finnish?

Agglutination yields combinatorially many word forms, so unseen ones always remain. This motivates subword modelling.

Which four English-first assumptions are built into standard NLP pipelines?

Words separated by whitespace; grammar encoded by word order; one word = one meaning; text flows left to right.

Which languages break the assumption that words are separated by whitespace?

Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer, Burmese.

Why do word-position features carry little information in Russian, Finnish or Korean?

Grammatical relations are marked by case on the noun, so word order is free.

Which two kinds of language break the assumption "one word = one unit of meaning"?

Agglutinative languages (Finnish, Turkish, Hungarian) and compounding languages (German, Dutch, Swedish).

In Arabic or Hebrew text, is the first character of the Unicode string the leftmost one on screen?

No. Codepoints are stored in logical order; the bidirectional algorithm renders them right to left.

Which type of writing system has each symbol represent a consonant or vowel sound?

Alphabetic (Latin, Cyrillic, Hangul), typically a small inventory.

What does each symbol represent in a syllabic writing system such as Hiragana or Katakana?

A whole syllable, a combination of consonant and vowel.

What does each symbol represent in a logographic writing system such as Chinese Hanzi?

A word or morpheme directly, with little sound information.

How does writing-system type affect a character-level vocabulary?

Alphabets need tens of symbols; logographic scripts need thousands, which the vocabulary must budget for.

What is a script called in which each consonant carries an inherent vowel that vowel signs override, as in Devanagari?

An abugida (alphasyllabic).

What is a script called that writes the consonantal skeleton and omits vowels, as in Arabic?

An abjad.

Why is the Arabic abjad a problem no tokenizer can fix?

One written form (كتب: kataba, kutiba, kutub) maps to several words; the vowels were never written, so context must disambiguate.

Why does Korean Hangul not fit neatly into the alphabetic / syllabic / logographic taxonomy?

Alphabetic by symbol-to-sound (each jamo a consonant or vowel), but jamo are grouped into square syllable blocks.

Why can a single "script of this text" label fail for a Japanese sentence?

One Japanese sentence mixes Katakana, Kanji and Hiragana.

Does detecting the script of a text identify its language?

No. Latin serves many languages; Arabic script covers Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Sorani.

Does knowing a text's language tell you its script?

No. Serbian is written in Cyrillic and Latin, sometimes in the same document.

What happens to a model trained on Cyrillic Serbian and tested on Latin Serbian?

It sees no shared characters, although the language is the same.

How many codes did ASCII define?

128: enough for English letters, digits and punctuation.

How did code pages extend ASCII?

By reinterpretation: the same 128 or 256 byte codes, read differently per language or script.

What are the two disadvantages of code pages?

Gibberish when decoded with the wrong table; one script per document.

Why does decoding text with the wrong code page usually raise no error?

The bytes are legal in both tables, so you silently get wrong characters (mojibake).

What is Unicode?

A single universal character set giving every character in every supported script one unique number, its codepoint (written U+number).

Does Unicode define how characters are stored as bytes?

No. Unicode defines meaning (codepoints); bytes are a separate encoding question, answered by the UTF encodings.

How does Unicode relate to ASCII?

Unicode is a superset: U+0000–U+007F are identical to ASCII.

How many codepoints does each Unicode plane contain?

65,536.

What follows from a codepoint lying outside plane 0, the Basic Multilingual Plane?

It needs more than 16 bits, so it takes four bytes in UTF-8, as emoji do.

What is the difference between a codepoint, a character and a glyph?

Codepoint: abstract number. Character: what a reader perceives, possibly several codepoints. Glyph: a stylistic rendering.

Why does a naive comparison fail on two identical-looking "café" strings?

One is NFC (é one precomposed codepoint), the other NFD (e plus combining accent): different sequences, lengths, hashes.

Where do byte, codepoint, character and grapheme coincide?

Only in ASCII, which is where the intuition was formed.

How does the Unicode stack run from what a reader sees down to what a byte-level tokenizer eats?

A grapheme; one or more codepoints; 1 to 4 bytes per codepoint.

Which Unicode normalization forms are lossy?

The compatibility forms NFKC and NFKD; the canonical forms NFC and NFD are reversible.

What does NFKD do that NFD does not?

Breaks characters into their most basic parts, e.g. splitting ligatures, normalizing visual styling away.

Which normalization form should you use to compare rendered text?

NFD (non-lossy, preserves semantic and visual equivalence).

Which normalization form should you use for search indexing or NLP analysis?

NFKD.

Why is NFKD's lossiness useful for search indexing?

It unifies variants: the ligature ﬁ matches fi, ① becomes 1.

Why should generation training targets not be NFKD-normalized?

The model can never learn to produce the character the user actually typed.

How many bytes per codepoint does UTF-8 use?

1 to 4, variable.

Which Unicode encoding uses exactly 4 bytes for every codepoint?

UTF-32: simple for indexing, memory-hungry, rare in practice.

Which of UTF-8, UTF-16 and UTF-32 is ASCII compatible?

Only UTF-8; the other two use at least two bytes per character.

How many UTF-8 bytes does a common CJK character such as 中 take?

3.

How many UTF-8 bytes does an emoji from the Supplementary Multilingual Plane take?

4.

In UTF-8, what does a byte starting with 0 signal?

A complete single-byte character (ASCII).

In UTF-8, what does a byte starting with 11 signal?

The first byte of a multi-byte sequence; the number of leading 1s gives its length.

What is a UTF-8 byte of the form 10xxxxxx?

A continuation byte inside a multi-byte sequence.

Why does 中 (U+4E2D, 15 significant bits) need the 3-byte UTF-8 form?

Its bits exceed the 11 payload bits of the two-byte form; the three-byte form carries sixteen.

Why is UTF-8 self-synchronising?

No byte is ambiguous about its role: from any offset you find a character start by skipping 10xxxxxx bytes.

How is byte inflation defined for byte-level tokenizers?

UTF-8 bytes of a string divided by its number of perceived characters.

Which has the highest byte inflation: "hello", "café" or "中文"?

中文: 3 bytes per character, against 1 per character for “hello”.

How does higher byte inflation affect byte-level tokenization?

Tokens are split more aggressively, so the same meaning costs more tokens.

Why is byte inflation a fairness problem?

CJK users pay more tokens for the same sentence: shorter effective context, more compute, less meaningful units.

Which preprocessing steps silently go wrong when crawled text mixes NFC and NFD?

Deduplication, search, string equality and prefix matching: all assume identical codepoint sequences.

What is the fix for crawled data that mixes normalization forms?

Consistent normalization in the pipeline, e.g. first decompose, then compose.

What goes wrong when breaking a string at the byte level?

Multi-byte codepoints get split into fragments that are not characters at all.

What goes wrong when breaking a string at the codepoint level?

A base letter can be separated from its combining diacritic (e + ◌́).

Why is grapheme-level breaking the expensive option?

Grapheme boundaries are very language-specific.

Where does mixing string-breaking strategies cause misalignment bugs?

Span annotation (SQuAD-style offsets shift or truncate) and counting across corpora.

What matters most when choosing a string-breaking convention for a pipeline?

Consistency: one convention enforced everywhere matters more than which level you pick.

What is the Turkish-I problem?

In Turkish, lowercase I is dotless ı, not i, so locale-blind .lower() changes the word.

Why can strings not be sorted alphabetically by codepoint?

Accented letters’ codepoints are not necessarily near their base letter’s, so å can land nowhere near a.

How do Swedish and German differ in sorting å, ä, ö?

Swedish puts them after z as separate letters; German interleaves them (ä as ae).

Why is punctuation normalization (“...” vs «...» vs 「...」) hard?

There is no simple solution: the mappings between traditions must be manually encoded.

How should a multilingual tokenizer match punctuation?

By Unicode general category P (\p{P}), not a hand-written class like [.,!?], which misses 。 and ।.

True or false: Unicode defines both each character's codepoint and how it is stored as bytes.

False. Unicode defines codepoints only; bytes are the job of an encoding such as UTF-8.

True or false: UTF-8 is ASCII compatible.

True. U+0000–U+007F are encoded as single bytes identical to ASCII.

True or false: UTF-16 encodes every codepoint in exactly 2 bytes.

False. 2 or 4 bytes; codepoints outside plane 0 need 4.

True or false: NFKD normalization is reversible.

False. Compatibility forms are lossy (ſ becomes s); only the canonical forms NFC and NFD are reversible.

True or false: NFKD normalization makes the ligature ﬁ and the pair fi identical.

True. Compatibility decomposition splits ligatures, which is why NFKD suits search indexing.

True or false: Detecting a text's script is enough to identify its language.

False. Arabic script alone covers Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Pashto and Sorani.

True or false: NFC and NFD versions of "café" render identically but are unequal as strings.

True. Same appearance, different codepoint sequences (4 against 5 codepoints).

True or false: Deduplicating a crawled corpus that mixes NFC and NFD raises an error on the mismatched copies.

False. It fails silently: both copies are kept and the numbers are just wrong.

True or false: A byte-level tokenizer splits Chinese text into more tokens than English text of the same meaning.

True. CJK characters take 3 UTF-8 bytes against about 1 for Latin, so splitting is more aggressive.

True or false: Whitespace tokenization works for Thai because Thai uses spaces between words.

False. Thai spaces end clauses or sentences, not words.

Multiple selection: which are true of Unicode normalization? (A) NFC and NFD are reversible (B) NFKD is the right form for generation targets (C) NFKD maps the long s ſ to s (D) NFD merges a base letter and combining mark into one codepoint

A and C. B is false: NFKD’s lossiness stops the model producing what users typed. D is false: NFD decomposes; NFC composes.

Multiple selection: which are true of UTF-8? (A) It uses 1 to 4 bytes per codepoint (B) The number of leading 1s in a lead byte gives the sequence length (C) Emoji fit in 3 bytes (D) From a random offset you cannot tell whether you are at a character start

A and B. C is false: plane-1 emoji need 4 bytes. D is false: UTF-8 is self-synchronising, continuation bytes start with 10.

Multiple selection: which are true of string breaking? (A) Byte-level breaking can produce fragments that are not characters (B) Codepoint-level breaking always keeps a diacritic with its base letter (C) Grapheme boundaries are language-specific (D) Mixing breaking strategies across corpora is harmless

A and C. B is false: e and a combining accent are two codepoints and can be split. D is false: spans misalign and counts become incomparable.

L03 Morphology and Word Formation

Flashcards

What is the difference between a vocabulary and a lexicon?

A vocabulary is an inventory of word forms; a lexicon also stores the meanings and functions of the words.

What is the difference between word segmentation and subword segmentation?

Word segmentation finds word boundaries in scripts that do not mark them (Chinese); subword segmentation splits already delimited words into smaller units (Finnish).

Why are hand-built lexical resources like WordNet a poor basis for word meaning at scale?

They are expensive (every relation entered by hand), incomplete, and language-specific.

What four properties define word embeddings?

Low dimensional, dense, continuous, and learned by performing a prediction task.

What is the difference between CBOW and Skip-Gram?

CBOW predicts the centre word from its context; Skip-Gram predicts each context word from the centre word.

How does the CBOW task differ from n-gram language modelling?

A language model sees only the left context; CBOW sees both sides, because its goal is embeddings and prediction is only the means.

Why is the CBOW network deliberately kept simple?

So that capacity goes into the embeddings themselves instead of into prediction quality.

Why is CBOW called "bag of words"?

Its projection layer sums the context vectors, and summation is commutative, so the order of the context words is lost.

Why does vocabulary size ∣V∣ drive the memory footprint of a neural model?

Weight matrices are fixed size, and the output layer is a ∣V∣×m matrix plus a softmax over ∣V∣ at every position.

What is a vocabulary short-list?

Keeping only the n most frequent words and mapping every rare word to a single <unk> token.

What is the main disadvantage of a short-list with <unk>?

All rare words get equal probability in a given context, because they are literally the same token.

Why is a realistic Russian word vocabulary about five times the size of the English one?

Russian marks case on every noun, so each lemma surfaces in many more distinct forms. Speakers do not know more concepts.

Why can a word-level model not see that move and moved are related?

They are unrelated indices, so the relation must be learned from data separately for every inflected pair.

How could morphology give a representation for an unseen word like hammered?

Compose it from known parts: an embedding for hammer plus what -ed does to a verb. Context cannot help, since the word was never seen.

Why is Chinese word segmentation hard even with a good dictionary?

The same character string often has several valid segmentations, and the characters alone do not decide between them.

How many possible segmentations does a string of n characters have?

2n−1: each of the n−1 internal gaps is a boundary or not. Too many to enumerate.

What does maximum matching do at each position of a character string?

It greedily takes the longest word found in a precompiled vocabulary.

What does the or l == 1 clause in maximum matching do?

Emits a single character even if it is not in the vocabulary, so the algorithm always terminates.

What happens to an out-of-vocabulary word under maximum matching?

It is silently chopped into single characters or greedily mismatched; nothing in the output signals it.

How does Backward Maximum Matching differ from Forward Maximum Matching?

It starts at the end, takes the substring ending at position i, and prepends each word so output stays in reading order.

With vocabulary {研究, 研究生, 生活, 生, 活}, what does Forward Maximum Matching return for 研究生活?

研究生 | 活

With vocabulary {研究, 研究生, 生活, 生, 活}, what does Backward Maximum Matching return for 研究生活?

研究 | 生活

Why can Forward and Backward Maximum Matching give different segmentations of the same string?

The string is genuinely ambiguous, and the greedy rule lets that leak through; only the scan direction differs. Neither algorithm is buggy.

How does bidirectional maximum matching decide when FMM and BMM disagree?

A tie-breaking heuristic: prefer fewer total words or fewer single-character words. If they agree, accept with high confidence.

Why can no direction-based heuristic segment correctly in general?

Some strings have several correct segmentations depending on meaning and context, so you need a method that weighs evidence.

How is word segmentation reformulated for statistical methods?

As sequence labeling: tag each character with its role in a word (BMES).

What do the BMES tags stand for?

Begin, Middle, End, Single.

Which BMES tag only occurs in words of three or more characters?

M (Middle), for an interior character.

After which BMES tags is a word boundary inserted?

After every E and every S.

What segmentation does the tagging 研/B 究/E 生/S 活/S give?

研究 | 生 | 活

In an HMM word segmenter, which sequence is hidden?

The BMES tag sequence, which generates the observed characters.

What is the direction of the emission probability in a BMES HMM?

P(character∣tag): the character given the tag.

Why can the BMES tag B never be followed by another B?

A word opened by B must continue with M or close with E before a new word can begin.

Why does even a first-order HMM do well at BMES tagging?

About half the transitions are structurally impossible, and the transition probabilities learn these zeros from data.

How are BMES HMM parameters estimated from a segmented corpus?

Maximum likelihood from counts: e.g. a transition is the tag-bigram count divided by the count of the previous tag.

What is the Viterbi recursion for BMES tagging?

score[i][t]=maxt′​score[i−1][t′]⋅P(t∣t′)⋅P(ci​∣t)

What are backpointers for in Viterbi?

They store each cell’s winning predecessor, so the best path is recovered by backtracing instead of recomputing paths.

How does the cost of Viterbi compare with scoring every tag sequence?

Viterbi is O(n∣T∣2), linear in sentence length; brute force is exponential, ∣T∣n.

Why does an HMM handle unseen words better than maximum matching?

It needs no fixed dictionary: it works over characters, so an unseen word is just a plausible tag path.

How does an HMM resolve segmentation ambiguity, compared with bidirectional matching?

With probabilistic evidence learned from data, instead of direction-based tie-breaking heuristics.

What does the first-order Markov assumption restrict in an HMM segmenter?

Each tag depends only on the previous tag and the current character, so there is no longer-range context.

Which model is motivated by the HMM's lack of longer-range context in word segmentation?

Conditional random fields (CRFs), which condition on arbitrary features of the whole input.

What is the difference between inflection and derivation?

Inflection keeps the part of speech and changes grammatical features; derivation changes the part of speech and/or the meaning.

perfect → imperfect keeps the part of speech. Is it inflection or derivation?

Derivation: it changes the meaning and yields a new dictionary entry.

What is compounding?

Combining several words into one word, e.g. German Tisch + Bein = Tischbein (table leg).

Why can no fixed vocabulary be closed over morphology?

Morphological processes are productive: they apply iteratively and to new combinations (doomscrolled), so new forms keep appearing.

Why do inflectional features multiply the number of word forms instead of adding to it?

Features are independent slots, so forms per lemma are the product of the slot sizes.

What is a morpheme?

The smallest meaning-carrying part of a word.

What is the difference between free and bound morphemes?

Free morphemes can stand alone as words (book); bound morphemes cannot (dis-, -ed).

Why do bound morphemes matter for segmentation?

They never appear as standalone tokens in a corpus, so a word-level system never sees them on their own.

Why can a circumfix like German ge-seh-en not be analysed by stripping a prefix or a suffix?

ge- and -en are one morpheme in two pieces, attached front and back simultaneously; neither half means “past participle” alone.

What is non-concatenative morphology?

Morphology that changes the root itself, instead of stringing morphemes together.

What is the name for a vowel change inside the root, as in German Buch → Bücher?

Apophony, a kind of non-concatenative morphology.

Why can no string-cutting segmenter fully handle non-concatenative morphology?

Segmenters cut contiguous pieces, but the change is inside the root: no cut in Bücher separates “book” from “plural”.

In an isolating language, what carries the grammatical relations?

Word order, with particles for additional modification.

Which morphological type has words that tend not to change form at all?

Isolating (Chinese, Vietnamese).

Which morphological type has each morpheme contribute one clear meaning, with visible boundaries?

Agglutinative (Turkish, Finnish).

Which morphological type has a single suffix encode several grammatical features that cannot be separated?

Fusional (Russian, Spanish, Arabic).

Which morphological type packs verb, subject, object and modifiers into a single word?

Polysynthetic (Inuktitut, Mohawk).

What distinguishes agglutinative from fusional morphology?

Separability: an agglutinative affix carries one feature; a fusional affix carries several fused features.

Why is "more morphemes per word" the wrong definition of agglutinative?

Fusional languages can be just as dense; the criterion is separability. Defining by density misclassifies Spanish.

What scale do isolating and polysynthetic languages sit at the two ends of?

How much meaning is packed into one word.

What are the morphemes of Turkish evlerinizden ("from your houses")?

ev-ler-iniz-den: house + plural + your + from.

What makes agglutinative morphology learnable for a segmenter?

Suffixes are separable and reusable across many nouns: find Finnish -ssa once, apply it everywhere.

Are agglutinative morpheme boundaries always clean at the character level?

No: attachment can change characters (Finnish kala → kaloissa). Boundaries are clean at the morpheme level.

What does the Russian suffix -ami in knigami ("with books") encode?

Instrumental case and plural, fused in one suffix with no separable part.

Does the Russian suffix -ami mark gender?

No: it is the instrumental plural for all three genders, since Russian neutralises gender in the plural.

How does Arabic root-and-pattern morphology form yaktubu ("he writes")?

The consonantal root k-t-b (“write”) is poured into a pattern template of prefixes and vowels, ya- C1 C2 -u- C3 -u.

Why is written Arabic hard for a tokenizer beyond its interleaved morphemes?

Short vowels are not written, so part of the information is absent from the string (yaktubu against yuktabu).

Why can Inuktitut words not be split back into morphemes by string matching?

Phonological rules at the boundaries rewrite the morphemes (-k-mut-uq surfaces as -mmuu-).

Why does no vocabulary size cover a polysynthetic language like Inuktitut?

A word is a sentence, so word types grow with the number of sentences, not morphemes.

What are the two ways a word-level vocabulary fails for morphologically rich languages?

Keep all forms and ∣V∣ explodes; keep an English-sized vocabulary and get massive OOV rates.

How does a fixed-size vocabulary shared across languages treat English versus Finnish?

English words stay whole while Finnish gets fragmented, so its sentences cost more tokens: a bias before training starts.

Why are morphological analysers not the general fix for vocabulary blow-up?

They are highly language dependent: one per language, each needing linguistic expertise.

What does subword tokenization guarantee?

A fixed vocabulary with zero OOV rate, by falling back to characters when nothing longer fits.

Do subword tokens line up with morphemes?

Not guaranteed: somewhat, in concatenative languages. They do not recover knig + ami or Arabic root + pattern.

True or false: CBOW preserves the order of the context words in its projection layer.

False. It sums the context vectors, and summation is commutative, so order is lost.

True or false: Forward and Backward Maximum Matching always produce the same segmentation given the same vocabulary.

False. On ambiguous strings they often disagree (研究生 | 活 against 研究 | 生活).

True or false: Maximum matching flags out-of-vocabulary words in its output.

False. OOV words are silently chopped into single characters or mismatched.

True or false: Viterbi decoding of BMES tags runs in time linear in the sentence length.

True. It is O(n∣T∣2), and ∣T∣=4 is fixed.

True or false: In a BMES HMM, the emission probability is the probability of the character given the tag.

True. Emission is P(ci​∣ti​), not the tag given the character.

True or false: An HMM word segmenter can be trained without a segmented corpus, at a cost in quality.

True. Unsupervised Baum-Welch (EM) training is possible, but quality drops.

True or false: Derivation always changes a word's part of speech.

False. It changes POS and/or meaning: perfect → imperfect keeps the adjective.

True or false: A fixed word vocabulary sized for English produces massive OOV rates for morphologically rich languages.

True. Each lemma has many surface forms, so most forms fall outside the vocabulary.

True or false: Agglutinative languages differ from fusional languages mainly in having more morphemes per word.

False. The difference is separability; fusional languages can be just as dense.

True or false: Subword segmentation recovers the morphemes of non-concatenative forms such as German Bücher.

False. It only cuts contiguous pieces, so it solves the concatenative case only.

Multiple selection: which are true of Forward Maximum Matching? (A) It takes the longest vocabulary word at each position (B) It improves as it sees more text (C) It always terminates because a single character is emitted as fallback (D) It needs no precompiled vocabulary

A and C. B: it has no mechanism to exploit data. D: it depends entirely on a precompiled vocabulary.

L04 Subword Segmentation

Flashcards

Which two properties of the training data determine how well a word is represented in NMT?

Its frequency in the training data and the number of different contexts it occurs in.

Which two word-formation processes produce many of the new surface forms a fixed word vocabulary misses?

Inflection (tall, taller, tallest) and compounding (Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaft).

What two kinds of unit can a subword be?

A character n-gram, or a morphologically meaningful unit (language dependent).

What happens to an unseen word under a word-level NMT vocabulary?

It collapses to <UNK>, and related forms like tall and tallest share nothing.

Why is a character-level vocabulary a poor fix for OOV words, even though its OOV rate is near zero?

Each symbol carries almost no meaning, and sequences become very long, which is slow and burns context.

What is the typical size range of a subword (BPE) vocabulary today?

About 30K to 128K merge operations.

Why do multilingual models sit at the 128K end of the subword vocabulary range?

A vocabulary covering many scripts needs more room before any single language gets a decent share of it.

What are the three weaknesses of hand-built FST morphological analyzers?

Extremely laborious to produce, totally language dependent, and no preference among ambiguous analyses.

Why are hand-built FST analyzers ruled out for multilingual NLP even though they are accurate?

They are totally language dependent: one expert-built analyzer per language does not scale to a hundred languages.

Which data-driven morphological segmenter is based on the Minimum Description Length principle?

Morfessor.

Minimum Description Length minimises the sum of which two costs?

The cost of the model itself plus the cost for the model to generate the data.

Why does Minimum Description Length push segmentation towards morpheme-like pieces?

Whole words make the model huge, characters make the data expensive; the optimum is reusable pieces, which is what morphemes are.

Which three simplifications do current data-driven subword segmenters make relative to morphological analysis?

No labelling (a split only), no character substitutions or deletions, strictly concatenative.

What two practical properties do data-driven subword segmenters gain from their simplifications?

No annotation or linguistic knowledge needed, and they can be fitted to a desired vocabulary size.

How do BPE and Unigram LM differ in the direction of vocabulary construction?

BPE starts from characters and merges upward; Unigram LM starts from a large vocabulary and prunes downward to size k.

Which pair does BPE merge at each training step?

The most frequent adjacent symbol pair, counted over word types weighted by their frequency.

What is BPE's only hyperparameter?

The number of merge operations, which sets the final vocabulary size.

Why is BPE training cheap?

It runs over the word-type vocabulary with frequencies, never over the whole corpus.

What does BPE's end-of-word marker </w> let it distinguish?

Word-final pieces from word-internal ones, which is what makes detokenization possible.

Is the </w> marker what stops BPE from merging pairs across two words?

No. Each word type is a separate vocabulary entry, so no pair ever spans two words anyway.

Why can two BPE tokenizers trained on the same corpus with the same merge count still differ?

Implementations break ties differently (first seen, lexicographic, constituent frequency), so “BPE, 32k merges” does not identify a tokenizer.

When counting BPE pairs in the tall man is taller than the tallest man, does than count towards t+a?

No. In than the t is followed by h.

Why does BPE trained on the tall man is taller than the tallest man end with talle + r where morphology says tall + er?

The greedy tall + e merge was locally most frequent and destroyed the boundary; BPE never undoes a merge.

How is a trained BPE model applied to a new word?

Split it into characters and apply the merges in the order they were learned.

What are the three major advantages of BPE reported by Sennrich et al.?

Smaller vocabulary, better translation quality, far fewer OOV items.

In Sennrich et al.'s English to German NMT, how do 300K/500K word-level systems compare with 60K-90K subword systems?

The best single and ensemble scores among the word-level and subword systems come from subword systems, despite their much smaller vocabularies.

Why does joint BPE, trained on the union of source and target languages, help translation?

A string is segmented identically on both sides, which helps the model copy names across.

Which segmentation method merges the pair maximising count(a)count(b)count(ab)​?

WordPiece.

What is the difference between BPE's and WordPiece's choice of which pair to merge?

BPE takes the highest raw count; WordPiece divides by the parts’ counts, preferring pairs surprisingly frequent together.

Why would WordPiece merge t + h less eagerly than BPE does?

t and h are each common on their own, so the denominator shrinks the score of the common pair th.

Which well-known encoder model uses WordPiece as its tokenizer?

BERT.

What is the difference between WordPiece's ## and BPE's </w> markers?

WordPiece marks continuation (##ing attaches left); BPE marks word end with </w>.

What are BPE's two structural limitations?

Greedy (a merge is never undone) and deterministic (one segmentation per word).

Why is BPE's greediness a problem for segmentation quality?

With no lookahead or undo, an early locally optimal merge can block a globally better segmentation.

Why does BPE's determinism rule out subword regularization?

A word always gets one segmentation, so there is no distribution to sample alternatives from.

Is SentencePiece a segmentation algorithm?

No. It is Google’s toolkit, implementing both BPE and Unigram LM.

What are the four properties of Unigram LM (Kudo)?

Considers all segmentations, optimises the best global one, supports multiple segmentations, top-down.

How does Unigram LM score a segmentation x=(x1​,…,xM​)?

P(x)=∏i​p(xi​): subwords are assumed independent.

How does Unigram LM choose the segmentation of a string X?

It takes the argmax of P(x) over all segmentations S(X), a global choice.

What is the seed vocabulary in Unigram LM vocabulary construction?

All substrings occurring more than once in the data, not crossing words: typically millions of candidates.

How is the unigram LM fitted during Unigram LM vocabulary construction?

By EM, with each string’s segmentation as a latent variable: expected subword counts, then renormalise.

What does the token loss Lt​ measure in Unigram LM vocabulary construction?

How much corpus likelihood drops if token t is removed and strings using it are re-segmented.

When pruning a Unigram LM vocabulary, should the tokens with the highest or smallest loss Lt​ be removed?

The smallest Lt​. Algorithm 2 as printed in MNLP says highest, which would prune the most valuable tokens.

Why does Unigram LM prune at most a fraction α of the vocabulary per round?

Each Lt​ assumes all other tokens remain; refitting between rounds lets remaining pieces absorb the deleted ones’ work.

Why does Kudo's Unigram LM never prune single-character tokens?

So every string stays segmentable and pruning cannot create OOVs.

How many segmentations does a string of n characters have?

2n−1: each of the n−1 gaps is either a cut or not.

What two ingredients are needed to score candidate segmentations?

Scores for segments and a way to combine them; in the unigram model, p(xi​) and multiplication.

Why can dynamic programming find the best Unigram LM segmentation?

Segments are independent, so the best split of the left part does not depend on the right part.

What does the sequence-cutting problem show about finding the best segmentation?

Neither extreme wins: the optimum is an interior split, found only by comparing whole configurations.

What are the two principles of dynamic programming?

Solve each sub-problem only once and store its solution: a time-memory trade-off.

What is the difference between top-down memoized and bottom-up dynamic programming in practice?

Same asymptotic run time; bottom-up avoids recursion limits, top-down computes only the sub-problems it needs.

In bottom-up DP segmentation, what does the cell m[i,j] hold?

The score of the best segmentation of the substring c[i..j].

With what value is each DP cell m[i,j] initialised before trying split points?

p(c[i..j]), the no-split option; zero if the substring is not in the vocabulary.

Why is the DP segmentation table filled by increasing span length?

Both halves of any split are shorter, so their best scores are already final.

What is the complexity of span-table DP segmentation in word length n?

O(n3) time and O(n2) space.

Why must DP segmentation under a unigram model run in log space?

Multiplying many probabilities underflows to zero, so all candidates tie and the argmax is meaningless.

In DP segmentation of cats, why can cat | s win even though cats is itself in the vocabulary?

p(cat)⋅p(s) exceeds p(cats): a rare whole word loses to two common pieces.

How can you get more than the single Viterbi segmentation?

Yen’s or Eppstein’s n-best algorithms, or re-run with the winning split disallowed.

What is subword regularization?

At training time, sample a segmentation (e.g. from the n-best list) instead of always using the Viterbi one.

Which technique retrofits sampled segmentations onto BPE by randomly skipping merges?

BPE-dropout.

What do standard BPE implementations assume about their input that SentencePiece does not?

Word boundary detection: a pre-tokenizer has already split the text into words.

How does SentencePiece treat its input?

As a raw stream of Unicode characters including spaces, with the space escaped as ▁.

What two consequences follow from SentencePiece's raw-stream input?

No language-specific pre-tokenizer is needed, and segmentation becomes lossless.

What is the desegmentation problem?

Tokens like [Hello] [world] [.] do not record where spaces were, so detokenizers guess with per-language rules.

How is SentencePiece output desegmented?

Join the tokens and replace ▁ with a space: no rules, exactly invertible.

Same data, same vocabulary size, identical pretraining and model; only the tokenizer differs.

Unigram LM (corrupt|ed, ious|ly); BPE produces frequency artefacts.

Its early merges went to generic frequent character clusters; Unigram LM picks vocabulary by usefulness.

It splits them like 184 + 8, so the model must rebuild the year from a meaningless prefix.

What does BPE do wrong when segmenting Japanese, compared with Unigram LM?

It glues particles (は, に) onto content words, destroying units the model needs.

BPE spends it on near-useless tokens (wasted embeddings); Unigram LM keeps more tokens of moderate frequency.

BPE: word-initial single capitals; Unigram LM: suffixes like s, ed, ing, ly.

The tokenizer is a hyperparameter invisible in English benchmarks and dominant outside them: control for segmentation.

Typos (sometimes) and unknown characters.

Why are unknown characters the real OOV problem for character-based subword vocabularies?

The base vocabulary holds only characters seen in training; an unseen emoji or script has nothing to fall back to.

How does byte-level BPE guarantee a zero OOV rate?

It runs on UTF-8 bytes; the base alphabet is fixed at 256 symbols, so any input is representable.

Which segmentation variant do GPT-2 and most later decoder-only models use?

Byte-level BPE.

They take more bytes per character, so merges are spent just getting back to characters.

UTF-8 encoding length; nothing about the language itself.

They have many more distinct, individually rarer word forms, so each gets split further.

APIs price by token, and the same content takes more tokens.

Otherwise the comparison measures the vocabulary size, not the algorithm.

L05 Static Embeddings

Flashcards

What is the distributional hypothesis behind both count-based and prediction-based word vectors?

A word is characterised by the contexts it occurs in (Firth: “the company it keeps”), so similar contexts mean similar meaning.

When building count-based context vectors, is a word inside the window but outside the context vocabulary Vc​ counted?

No. A word counts only if it is both inside the window and in Vc​.

Why is word similarity measured with cosine rather than the raw dot product?

Cosine ignores vector length, so a frequent word’s large counts do not dominate; only the proportions of contexts matter.

What are the three properties of count-based context vectors?

High-dimensional (one dimension per context word), discrete (integer counts), sparse (mostly zeros).

What is the main weakness of count-based context vectors besides sparsity?

No explicit criterion to separate signal from noise: chance co-occurrences, and words like the that discriminate nothing.

How does idf weighting, log(N/nw​), help count-based context vectors?

A context word that occurs in nearly every context gets idf close to 0, so non-discriminative words are effectively removed.

What four properties distinguish prediction-based word embeddings from count-based context vectors?

Low-dimensional, dense, continuous, and learned by performing a prediction task. Their dimensions are not interpretable.

What is the difference between the CBOW and Skip-gram tasks?

CBOW predicts the centre word from its context. Skip-gram predicts each context word from the centre word.

Why is CBOW useless as a language model?

It also uses the words to the right of the target; an n-gram language model uses left context only.

How is the CBOW hidden layer h computed from the context words?

As the average of the 2n context words’ embeddings: h=2n1​WwC​, with no non-linearity.

Why is CBOW called a "bag of words" model?

The context word vectors are summed, so word order is lost.

What independence assumption does Skip-gram make?

The context words are independent given the input (centre) word.

Do Skip-gram's 2n output layers produce different distributions?

No. They share W′ and h, so the distribution is identical; only the target each is scored against differs.

What task replaces full-vocabulary prediction in negative sampling?

Binary classification: did this (word, context) pair come from the data or from a noise distribution?

How does negative sampling change the per-example cost of Word2Vec training?

From ∣V∣ dot products to k+1.

In negative sampling, what is the probability that a (word, context) pair is real?

σ(vc​⋅vw​): the sigmoid of the dot product of the context (output) and word (input) vectors.

Why are Word2Vec negatives drawn from P(w)0.75 rather than the raw unigram distribution?

To bias sampling towards rarer words; otherwise nearly every negative would be the, of or and.

What weakness of prediction-based methods like Word2Vec does GloVe address?

They treat each context window as an independent event, repeatedly rediscovering the same association.

Which tends to perform better: count-based or prediction-based word representations?

Prediction-based. Count-based methods use global information directly but tend to perform worse.

What does GloVe combine?

Global co-occurrence statistics (counted once) with local prediction-based gradient updating.

In GloVe's weighted least-squares objective, what target is wi​⋅w~j​+bi​+b~j​ fitted to?

The log co-occurrence count logXij​.

What is the purpose of GloVe's weighting function f(Xij​)?

To de-emphasise very rare (noisy) and very frequent (uninformative) co-occurrences.

What are the typical parameters of GloVe's weighting function?

xmax​=100, α=0.75.

What is the difference between Word2Vec and GloVe in what they train on?

Word2Vec: individual local context windows. GloVe: the global co-occurrence matrix, built in one pass over the corpus.

How do Word2Vec and GloVe differ in handling frequency imbalance?

Word2Vec subsamples frequent words. GloVe uses the explicit weighting function f(Xij​).

What is the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic evaluation of word embeddings?

Intrinsic: how good the embeddings are by themselves. Extrinsic: how useful they are in downstream tasks (MT, IR, summarisation).

How is word similarity evaluated against human judgements?

Rank word pairs by cosine and compare with the human ratings using Spearman (rank) correlation.

What is the difference between the WS-353 and SimLex-999 benchmarks?

WS-353 annotates relatedness (coffee/cup count as close). SimLex-999 annotates similarity.

How is the answer to "a is to b as c is to X" computed with word embeddings?

The word closest by cosine to vb​−va​+vc​, excluding the three input words. Scored by accuracy.

What is the difference between PCA and t-SNE for visualising embeddings?

PCA: linear projection onto the top-variance directions. t-SNE: non-linear, keeps local neighbours, gives up global distances.

Why should 2D t-SNE plots of embeddings not be taken at face value?

Non-linear projections distort distances, and t-SNE hyperparameter settings substantially change cluster sizes, distances, even apparent clusters.

Why do morphologically rich languages suffer most from one vector per word type?

Each lemma has many inflected forms, each a separate, rarer word with an unrelated, worse-estimated vector.

How does fastText compute a word's vector?

As the sum of the vectors of its character n-grams, plus the whole word.

What character n-gram lengths does fastText use by default?

3 to 6.

Why does fastText wrap each word in < and >?

The boundary markers make prefixes and suffixes distinct n-grams: <ru is only word-initial, ns> only word-final.

How does fastText's training differ from Skip-gram with negative sampling?

Only the centre word is decomposed into n-grams. The loss and the per-word context vectors are unchanged.

Where does fastText gain most on word similarity over Word2Vec?

Most in morphologically rich languages (German, Russian); it also helps rare words (English Rare Words).

Why do semantic analogies not benefit from fastText's character n-grams?

Relations like capital and country have nothing to do with character overlap; shared n-grams can add noise.

What is building a bilingual dictionary by nearest-neighbour search in a cross-lingual embedding space called?

Bilingual lexicon induction (BLI).

What are the three basic set-ups for training cross-lingual embeddings?

Train separately, then align the spaces; train together, then align regions; train on word- or sentence-aligned data.

What does the shared space hypothesis claim?

Separately trained language spaces are approximately isomorphic: same overall shape, possibly different coordinates and orientation.

Why is the cross-lingual mapping assumed to be linear?

Analogies are already linear regularities within one space, and a linear map preserves them exactly across spaces.

What bilingual signal does each type of cross-lingual alignment use?

Supervised: thousands of seed translations. Semi-supervised: hundreds, then bootstrapping. Unsupervised: none (adversarial training).

What is the closed-form solution of the unconstrained least-squares cross-lingual mapping minW​∑i​∥Wxi​−yi​∥2?

W∗=YX⊤(XX⊤)−1.

What is the difference between solving the least-squares mapping in closed form and with SGD?

Closed form is exact. SGD is approximate but scales better and is more memory-friendly; Mikolov et al. used SGD.

In cross-lingual results tables, what does "Procrustes" refer to?

The orthogonally constrained mapping solved by SVD. The unconstrained map is Mikolov et al.’s translation matrix.

How does linear-mapping translation accuracy change for rarer words?

It drops: rarer words have worse embeddings, so their mapped positions are less reliable.

Why does an unconstrained mapping W overfit the seed dictionary?

It can stretch, shear and rotate the space to fit the seed pairs, so it generalises poorly to other words.

What mismatch between training and inference does the unconstrained mapping have?

Training minimises Euclidean distance, retrieval uses cosine. They agree only for unit-normalised vectors.

What constraint and normalisation define Xing et al.'s orthogonal mapping?

W⊤W=I, with all embeddings normalised to unit length.

What transformations can an orthogonal mapping W perform?

Only rotations and reflections: no scaling or shearing, so angles and distances are preserved.

How is the orthogonal Procrustes mapping solved?

Exactly by SVD: UΣV⊤=SVD(YX⊤), then W=UV⊤.

Why does the unconstrained mapping get worse as the embedding dimension grows?

W has d2 free parameters for the same seed dictionary, so there is more room to overfit.

How does the orthogonal mapping compare with the unconstrained one across embedding dimensions?

Better at every dimension, and it improves slightly as dimension grows.

What is a hub in a high-dimensional embedding space?

A vector that is the nearest neighbour of many points regardless of actual similarity.

What is the CSLS score between a mapped source vector x and a target vector y?

CSLS(x,y)=2cos(x,y)−rT​(x)−rS​(y).

Which CSLS term penalises hubs?

rS​(y): the candidate’s average cosine to its K nearest mapped source vectors.

Why does rT​(x) not change which translation CSLS picks for a given source word?

It is the same for every candidate y. It matters only when comparing scores across source words.

How does CSLS retrieval compare with plain nearest neighbour on the same Procrustes mapping?

CSLS is better on every language pair, with no tuning beyond K.

What is the chicken-and-egg problem of unsupervised cross-lingual alignment?

You need a mapping to find translation pairs, and translation pairs to learn the mapping.

In MUSE's adversarial training, what does the discriminator try to distinguish?

Mapped source vectors Wx from real target vectors y. The generator is the mapping W.

Why is MUSE's generator a single linear matrix rather than a deep network?

A deep generator could match the target distribution while scrambling which word maps where.

Why does MUSE use label smoothing ( s=0.2) on the discriminator?

To stop it becoming overconfident, which would give the generator vanishing gradients.

Why does MUSE feed the discriminator only the 50k most frequent words?

Rare words have poorly estimated embeddings, so frequent words give a cleaner signal.

What does re-orthogonalisation do in MUSE's adversarial training?

After each gradient step it pulls W back towards an orthogonal matrix (β=0.01).

What unsupervised criterion does MUSE use to select a checkpoint?

The average cosine between the 10k most frequent source words and their CSLS nearest target words.

How does MUSE build its synthetic dictionary for refinement?

From mutual CSLS nearest neighbours among frequent words, keeping only high-confidence pairs.

Why does MUSE's synthetic dictionary keep only mutual nearest neighbours?

A pair is trusted only if each word picks the other, which filters out hub matches.

Why does the MUSE refinement loop count as semi-supervised?

It runs supervised Procrustes on a dictionary the system built itself. Seeded with a small human dictionary, it is the semi-supervised method.

How does fully unsupervised MUSE compare with supervised Procrustes-CSLS for close pairs (en-es, en-fr, en-de)?

It matches or slightly beats supervised alignment.

How does fully unsupervised MUSE compare with supervised alignment for distant pairs (en-ru, en-zh)?

It falls well short.

What is the difference between head-marking and dependent-marking?

Head-marking puts the grammatical marker on the head. Dependent-marking puts it on the dependent.

Which language pairs did adversarial alignment fail on completely in Søgaard et al. (2018)?

EN-ET, EN-FI, EN-EL: Estonian, Finnish and Greek, the mixed- or double-marking languages.

What is identical-word supervision for cross-lingual alignment?

A seed dictionary of words spelled identically in both languages. It needs no human dictionary.

What does the Estonian-Finnish result show about unsupervised alignment?

Structurally similar languages align adversarially even when neither aligns with English. It is the only pair in Søgaard et al.’s language comparison where adversarial beats identical.

Why does unsupervised alignment fail for mixed- and double-marking languages, according to Søgaard et al.?

Each lemma has many distinct surface forms, each its own lower-frequency embedding, so the mapping becomes non-isomorphic.

Is the English-Finnish alignment failure caused by too little Finnish training data?

No. Retraining on a far larger Finnish corpus still gave zero precision.

How does a domain mismatch between the two corpora affect identical-word supervision and adversarial alignment?

Identical-word supervision degrades but survives. Adversarial alignment collapses to about zero.

How much do hyperparameter changes within one embedding algorithm (skipgram window, n-gram range) hurt unsupervised alignment?

Barely: drops of under two points.

What happens to unsupervised alignment when one language uses skipgram embeddings and the other CBOW?

It fails completely (about zero), even with otherwise identical hyperparameters.

How is eigenvector similarity Δ computed for two languages?

Sum of squared differences between the largest Laplacian eigenvalues of their nearest-neighbour graphs.

What does a larger eigenvector similarity Δ mean?

The two graphs are more structurally different: further from isomorphism.

Why does eigenvector similarity not need a bilingual dictionary?

Laplacian eigenvalues are invariant to relabelling the nodes, so no alignment between the languages is needed.

Why can no rotation fix a high eigenvector similarity Δ?

A rotation preserves the nearest-neighbour graph, so it cannot change the Laplacian eigenvalues.

Why does Finnish give a high eigenvector similarity Δ against English?

One lemma’s inflected forms cluster tightly, while English gives a flatter, more evenly connected graph.

How does eigenvector similarity Δ relate to adversarial alignment success?

It tracks it: the lowest-Δ pairs align best, and the highest-Δ pairs fail completely.

What does "static" mean for Word2Vec, GloVe and fastText embeddings?

One vector per word type, whatever the sentence: bank is the same in river bank and bank account.

False. That is CBOW. Skip-gram’s hidden layer is the centre word’s embedding.

True or false: Word2Vec's context size and number of negative samples can be smaller the more training data there is.

True. More data allows a smaller context and a smaller negative sample set.

True or false: fastText can produce a vector for a word that never occurred in training.

True. It sums the vectors of the word’s character n-grams.

True or false: fastText improves over Word2Vec on both semantic and syntactic analogies.

False. The gains are syntactic; semantic analogies do not improve and sometimes get worse.

True or false: For unit-length vectors and an orthogonal W, minimising Euclidean distance is equivalent to maximising cosine similarity.

True. ∥Wx−y∥2=2−2cos(Wx,y).

True or false: The unconstrained least-squares mapping continuously improves as the embedding dimension grows.

False. It gets worse, since d2 parameters overfit the same dictionary. The orthogonal map improves slightly.

False. It can look fine while the mapping is poor; MUSE selects by average CSLS-neighbour cosine instead.

False. Across domains it collapses to about zero, even for English-Spanish.

True. From a small margin (EN-ES) to working against not working (EN-ET).

False. Near-isomorphic spaces give a low Δ, but a low Δ does not imply near-isomorphism.

L06 Contextual Embeddings

Flashcards

What is the shared flaw of static word embeddings, however they are trained?

One vector per word type: train (vehicle), train (to practise) and train of thought all get the same vector.

Why do embeddings taken from a trained task model's input layer (e.g. an MT system's) still lack context?

The network saw the whole sentence, but only the input embedding layer is kept, so the context computed in deeper layers is thrown away.

What trade-off must a contextual word representation strike?

Enough context to separate occurrences of the same word, but not so much that the word’s own contribution becomes unclear.

Name the three strategies for balancing word and context information in contextual representations.

Choosing the right layer(s), choosing the right task(s), tightening connections between layers (e.g. residual connections).

What is the difference between using a pre-trained encoder as a feature extractor and full fine-tuning?

Feature extractor: encoder frozen, only the task head trained. Full fine-tuning: head and encoder updated (BERT’s default).

What fraction of tokens does BERT select for masked language modelling?

15%.

In BERT pre-training, what happens to a token selected for masking?

80% replaced by [MASK], 10% by a random word, the rest left unchanged.

At which positions is BERT's MLM loss computed?

Only at the selected positions, whether they show [MASK], a random word or the original word.

Why does BERT not replace every selected token with [MASK]?

[MASK] never appears at fine-tuning time; random and unchanged tokens force good representations of every input token.

How are BERT's next sentence prediction pairs built?

50% B truly follows A (IsNext), 50% B is a random segment (NotNext).

Which BERT special token is never masked and has a final output representing the whole input sequence?

[CLS].

From which output does BERT predict next sentence prediction?

C, the final-layer output at the [CLS] position.

How many attention heads does BERT-base have?

12. A deck slide gives base 16 heads, which is an error: that is BERT-large’s count.

When fine-tuning BERT, where does the task head attach for classification versus tagging?

Classification: the [CLS] output C. Tagging and span extraction: the token outputs Ti​.

Which cross-lingual signals does mBERT's training lack?

Parallel data, language-ID embedding, language-specific parameters, alignment loss.

Since mBERT's training objective is not cross-lingual, what do its languages share?

Only the parameters and the subword vocabulary.

Why does mBERT sample languages with a smoothed distribution?

Wikipedia sizes are extremely skewed: English alone is larger than dozens of small languages combined.

What goes wrong with language sampling at α = 0?

Sampling becomes uniform: tiny languages are oversampled so much that the model memorises them.

What does language sampling with α = 0.7 do relative to raw data sizes?

Up-weights low-resource and down-weights high-resource languages: a compromise between proportional and uniform.

Why does an exponent α < 1 in pl​∝nlα​ shrink the gap between languages?

xα is concave and compresses large values more: the ratio n1​/n2​ becomes (n1​/n2​)α.

How large is mBERT's shared vocabulary compared with English BERT's?

120k entries against 30k.

Why does mBERT have more parameters than BERT-base?

Entirely the bigger vocabulary (embedding matrix); the architecture is identical.

Even with smoothing, which languages does mBERT's shared vocabulary favour?

High-resource languages and Latin script.

Which languages get high fertility under mBERT's shared vocabulary?

Low-resource and morphologically rich languages: many more pieces per word.

Why is uncased mBERT bad for some languages?

It lowercases and strips accents, damaging languages that depend on diacritics.

Which segmentation method merges the pair maximising count(a,b) / (count(a) · count(b))?

WordPiece.

What is the difference between BPE's and WordPiece's merge criterion?

BPE merges the most frequent pair; WordPiece the pair with the highest count(a,b) / (count(a) · count(b)).

Why can WordPiece merge a rare pair before a frequent one?

The score is high when the pair occurs together more often than its parts’ frequencies suggest; pairs containing a very common symbol score low.

What does WordPiece do with a word containing a character missing from its vocabulary?

The whole word becomes [UNK], where BPE and Unigram LM fall back to characters.

Why does per-token random masking weaken MLM when words are split into subwords?

Morphological pieces (suffixes) are trivially recoverable from the visible stem, so many masked positions teach little.

Which masking scheme masks all subword pieces of a word together, at an unchanged masking rate?

Whole-word masking.

What is zero-shot cross-lingual transfer?

Fine-tune on task data in language A only, evaluate on language B with no task data in B.

Does mBERT transfer between languages with different scripts, such as Hindi and Urdu?

Yes, well, despite almost no shared wordpieces.

Why does mBERT's zero-shot POS transfer drop for pairs involving Japanese?

Possibly larger typological differences (Japanese is SOV with postpositions).

How does zero-shot NER F1 depend on entity-wordpiece overlap for mBERT versus English BERT?

mBERT: roughly flat, decent even near zero overlap. English BERT: near zero at low overlap, rising with overlap.

What does mBERT's flat zero-shot F1 against wordpiece overlap imply?

Its transfer is not surface string matching: representations are shared at a level deeper than surface wordpieces.

How does mBERT's zero-shot POS transfer depend on word-order type (SVO, SOV)?

Best within the same type: SVO to SOV is far worse than SVO to SVO.

Which typological database records structural features of languages and is used in NLP to measure structural similarity?

WALS (World Atlas of Language Structures).

How does zero-shot POS accuracy change as two languages share more WALS features?

It rises, for both mBERT and English BERT (mBERT higher throughout).

Why does mBERT translation retrieval get worse in the top layers?

Top layers are shaped by predicting words in a specific language, so they become language-specific again.

For mBERT on XNLI, which is better: machine-translated training data (pseudo-supervision) or zero-shot transfer?

Pseudo-supervision (translate-train) beats zero-shot.

On zero-shot XNLI, how does mBERT compare with systems pretrained with cross-lingual signal such as bitext?

It loses to the strong ones (Artetxe and Schwenk; XLM with MLM+TLM) but beats the older X-LSTM.

Does zero-shot mBERT beat a POS tagger trained on a few hundred target-language sentences?

No, not on average: for POS a small amount of target-language data is worth more.

In Wu and Dredze's experiment, how does Feat (mBERT not fine-tuned, only the task head trained) compare with fine-tuning?

Worse on average on every task than fine-tuning with only the embeddings frozen: fine-tuning is necessary.

Why does freezing mBERT's lower layers during English fine-tuning help zero-shot transfer?

English-only fine-tuning would pull them toward English; frozen, they keep their multilingual pre-trained representation.

What happens to mBERT zero-shot transfer if layers up to 9 are frozen during fine-tuning?

It hurts NER (most), POS and XNLI, though not MLDoc: the upper layers must adapt to the task.

For which task does wordpiece overlap with English correlate most strongly with mBERT's zero-shot score?

NER, almost perfectly: the most lexical task, with entities often copied verbatim.

Why does a correlation between wordpiece overlap and transfer not show that overlap causes transfer?

High-overlap languages are also typologically closer to English, so the two are confounded.

What is fake English (K et al. 2020)?

English with every Unicode codepoint shifted by a large constant, so it shares no characters with the other language.

Why does fake English isolate the effect of subword overlap?

Words, grammar, word order and frequencies stay identical; only the characters change, so shared vocabulary is zero.

How much XNLI transfer does B-BERT lose when fake English removes all subword overlap?

Very little: about one point.

How did K et al. test whether word order matters for cross-lingual transfer?

Randomly permuting words during pre-training, with no permutation during fine-tuning.

Of Spanish, Hindi and Russian, which loses most transfer when B-BERT is pre-trained with permuted words?

Hindi: SOV, the furthest from English word order.

Which model-architecture factor has the largest impact on B-BERT's cross-lingual transfer?

Depth.

As B-BERT gets deeper at roughly constant size, which improves more: in-language or zero-shot accuracy?

Zero-shot keeps improving; in-language saturates early, so the gap shrinks.

What does the mixed-language XNLI drop (premise and hypothesis in different languages) show about mBERT-style models?

The space is not truly language-neutral: good at a task in another language, poor at relating two languages in one input.

What is the difference between "within" and "across" in Rajaee and Monz's XNLI evaluation?

Within: premise and hypothesis in the same language. Across: in different languages.

What is the heuristics hypothesis (Rajaee and Monz)?

Transfer of heuristics can contribute to cross-lingual generalisation.

Which shortcut does SNLI training data offer an NLI model?

Full premise-hypothesis word overlap almost always means entailment; low overlap mostly non-entailment.

Why would an NLI word-overlap shortcut fail when premise and hypothesis are in different languages?

Lexical overlap is near zero by construction, so the shortcut predicts non-entailment. (An interpretation, not an established result.)

Why does XLM-R drop next sentence prediction and train with MLM only?

NSP contributes little and sometimes hurts performance.

What is the difference between static and dynamic masking?

Static: masked once in preprocessing, reused every epoch. Dynamic: a fresh mask each time a sequence is fed.

Why is dynamic masking better than static masking?

Under static masking unselected tokens are never targets; dynamic masking gives new training signal each pass at no data cost.

What data is XLM-R trained on?

CC-100: filtered CommonCrawl in 100 languages, two orders of magnitude more than mBERT’s Wikipedia.

What vocabulary does XLM-R use?

250k entries, Unigram LM via SentencePiece: better fertility, bigger embedding matrix.

What does BERT output for SQuAD question answering?

A start/end span in the paragraph; nothing is generated.

Which new parameters does BERT learn when fine-tuned for SQuAD?

Only a start vector S and an end vector E, scored against each token output by inner product.

What structure does XLM-R's cross-lingual QA (context by question language) matrix show?

“Own language, or English question”: both work, little in between.

What is the bottom line on what drives mBERT's cross-lingual transfer?

No single factor: vocabulary overlap plays a minor role, structure and depth matter.

Which training signal, absent from mBERT, could improve its cross-lingual transfer?

Parallel data with an alignment loss pulling translation equivalents close together.

True. Still about 15% of tokens; only the choice of which tokens changes.

False. It costs only about one XNLI point.

True. Accuracy drops, but stays well over chance.

L07 Crosslingual NLP

Flashcards

Which of the four senses in which mBERT and XLM-R are "multilingual" leaves languages unlinked?

Their predictions are based on context within the same language (MLM).

What is crosslingual knowledge transfer?

Fine-tuning on task data in language A, then testing on the same task in language B.

What is crosslingual transfer called when no task data in the test language was seen at all?

Zero-shot crosslingual transfer.

Why does training on many languages not by itself link them?

Predicting word W in language L gains nothing from unrelated L′ context: languages share parameters, never context.

What is the difference between parallel and comparable data?

Parallel: meaning-equivalent pairs (translations). Comparable: same topic, parallel only to a degree on a sliding scale.

What are the two steps of crawling your own parallel corpus?

Document alignment, then sentence alignment inside the aligned documents.

Why is sentence alignment a separate step after document alignment?

Parallel segments do not follow paragraph boundaries, so links other than paragraph-to-paragraph are needed.

How does the LASER encoder turn a sentence into one vector?

BPE embeddings, a stack of BiLSTMs, then max pooling over time into a fixed-size sentence embedding.

Why are LASER sentence embeddings language-independent?

The decoder sees the source only through the sentence vector and gets the output language from a language ID embedding, so the encoder need encode only meaning.

Which sentence alignment method matches sentences by LASER embedding similarity, with a search that scales to massive data sets?

vecalign.

What does XLM add that mBERT lacks?

An explicit crosslingual loss on parallel data. mBERT never sees a translation pair, so its alignment is an emergent side effect.

What are XLM's three pretraining objectives?

CLM, MLM, TLM.

Which XLM objective masks words in a sentence concatenated with its translation?

TLM, translation language modeling.

How does TLM differ from MLM?

The loss is the same; only the input differs: a translation pair instead of monolingual text.

Why does TLM force crosslingual alignment?

A masked word can be predicted from its unmasked translation in the other language, so the model learns to attend across languages.

What is the difference between translate-train and translate-test on XNLI?

Translate-train machine-translates the English training set into each language; translate-test translates each test set into English.

Does zero-shot XLM (MLM+TLM) beat the translate-test pipeline on XNLI?

Yes, slightly, without ever seeing non-English NLI data.

Which languages gain most on XNLI when TLM is added to MLM?

Those distant from English in script or structure (e.g. vi, tr, ar, zh). English itself gains least.

For XLM language modeling of Nepali, does adding Hindi or English data help more?

Hindi, the related language, by far. Adding both is best.

What are InfoXLM's three objectives?

MMLM, TLM, XLCo.

What does InfoXLM's XLCo objective classify?

Whether a sentence pair is parallel, from the two [CLS] representations, with random sentences as negatives.

What is the difference between the crosslingual signal of TLM and of XLCo?

TLM aligns at the token level; XLCo aligns whole-sentence representations.

What is the difference between the within and across scores in mixed-language XNLI and QA?

Within: both input parts in language ℓ. Across: mean over mixed-language combinations with ℓ in either part.

What is paradoxical about XLM-R against mBERT in mixed-language evaluation?

XLM-R is much better within languages but worse across languages on QA.

How does InfoXLM compare with XLM-R on within and across scores?

Only slightly better within, much better across: the within-across gap shrinks sharply.

Why does crosslingual QA expose purely multilingual models more than XNLI?

Extractive QA needs word-level matching of question to context across languages; XNLI can rely on a coarse sentence gist.

What do mixed-language evaluations show about crosslingual ability without a crosslingual training signal?

It largely does not emerge: standard zero-shot benchmarks make multilingual models look more crosslingual than they are.

What two assumptions of sequence labeling does sequence-to-sequence modeling drop?

That xt​ corresponds to yt​, and that ∣X∣=∣Y∣.

How is sequence-to-sequence modeling written as conditional language modeling?

p(yt​∣Y<t​,X): the next output token given the decoder prefix and the encoder output.

Which four complex mappings make machine translation hard?

Different lengths, word order differences, one-to-many, many-to-one.

How did Sutskever et al. (2014) connect the LSTM encoder and decoder?

By initialising the decoder LSTM with the encoder’s last state.

What problem does h0dec​=hnenc​ cause in an RNN encoder-decoder?

An information bottleneck: the whole source sentence must pass through one fixed-size vector.

How do the NMT decoder input x and the target y differ?

x = <s> + sentence, y = sentence + </s>: the same sentence shifted by one.

Which Transformer decoder component replaces the RNN encoder-decoder bottleneck?

The source-target context layer (cross-attention) over all top-layer encoder outputs, in every decoder layer.

With small parallel corpora, which wins: NMT or statistical MT?

Statistical MT. NMT is data hungry: it starts far lower and only overtakes with much more data.

What is parent-child transfer learning for NMT?

Train on a high-resource pair (parent), then continue training the same model on the low-resource pair (child).

Which conditions make parent-child NMT transfer work best?

Same target language, related source languages, frozen target side (really only target embeddings), shared vocabulary. Kocmi and Bojar’s unrelated parents and Aji et al.’s En→De parent weaken the first two.

In parent-child NMT transfer, which parameters should stay frozen?

Only the target-language embeddings. Training the target RNN and attention still helps, so “freeze the decoder” overstates it.

Why does unfreezing the target embeddings hurt a low-resource NMT child?

The parent learned them from far more target-language data; retraining them on the small child data overfits.

Which child language gains most from parent-child transfer in Zoph et al. (2016)?

The one with the smallest corpus (Urdu).

Does a related parent help NMT transfer more than an unrelated one in Zoph et al. (2016)?

Yes: a Spanish child gains more from a French parent than from a German one.

What does NMT transfer to French' (French with randomly reshuffled vocabulary) show?

Transfer is not only about shared words: structure such as word order and syntax transfers too.

With a shared parent-child vocabulary, do unrelated parents help NMT transfer (Kocmi and Bojar, 2018)?

Yes, about as much as related ones. Having a large parent matters most.

According to Aji et al. (2020), which part of a parent NMT model carries most of the transfer benefit?

The inner (non-embedding) layers.

According to Aji et al. (2020), how do parent embeddings transferred alone compare with training the child from scratch?

Worse on average: without the parent’s inner layers they are a bad initialisation.

What is the difference between parent-child transfer and multilingual NMT?

Parent-child transfers knowledge in stages; multilingual NMT transfers it in parallel through joint training.

Which multilingual NMT language combination is used for testing only?

Many-to-many. Many-to-one and one-to-many are used in training.

What is translating in a language direction never seen during training called?

Zero-shot translation.

What are the two indicators that knowledge transfer happens in multilingual NMT?

Performance on zero-shot directions, and beating a bilingual baseline trained only on that direction’s data.

What does a tag like <2es> name in Johnson et al.'s multilingual NMT?

The target language. Nothing names the source language.

Why does many-to-one multilingual NMT help more consistently than one-to-many?

Many-to-one shares one target side; in one-to-many the decoder produces several languages that compete for parameters.

What is it called when a direction gets worse because other languages compete for the same model capacity?

Interference.

Do low-resource languages gain more from multilingual NMT when translating into English or out of it?

Into English (many-to-one).

Which sampling temperature gives proportional sampling?

T=1.

Which sampling temperature is the compromise between proportional and uniform in Arivazhagan et al. (2019)?

T=5.

What does training on more languages (10 against 102) do to zero-shot translation?

Improves most directions, not all; it depends on the languages, and zero-shot still lags behind.

What is BART's architecture?

A bidirectional encoder (as BERT) plus an autoregressive decoder (as GPT).

What is BART's pretraining task?

Reconstruct the original document from a corrupted one: a denoising autoencoder.

What are BART's five noise functions?

Token masking, token deletion, text infilling, sentence permutation, document rotation.

What is the difference between BART's token masking and token deletion?

Masking marks the gap with [MASK]; deletion leaves no mark, so the model must find where tokens are missing.

What extra thing must BART work out under text infilling?

How many tokens the single [MASK] stands for.

Which single BART noise function works best?

Text infilling.

How do sentence permutation and document rotation perform as BART's only noise function?

Poorly: rearranging whole sentences gives too weak a token-level learning signal.

How is BART fine-tuned for classification?

The same input goes to encoder and decoder; the label is predicted from the last decoder hidden state.

How is BART adapted for machine translation into English?

A randomly initialised source encoder replaces the embedding layer of BART’s encoder.

What is the difference between Fixed BART and Tuned BART for Romanian→English MT?

Fixed (BART frozen) is worse than the baseline; Tuned (BART updated) beats it.

Which noise functions does mBART's pretraining use?

Text infilling plus sentence permutation.

What is the difference between translating with BART and with mBART?

BART (English-only) needs a new source encoder; mBART is fine-tuned as is on parallel data.

At what amount of parallel data does mBART pretraining help translation most?

Low to medium (a few hundred thousand pairs). Almost no data cannot be rescued, and gains shrink with more data.

True or false: TLM uses a different loss function from MLM.

False. The loss is the same; only the input (a sentence plus its translation) differs.

True or false: In TLM, the position embeddings restart at 0 for the second sentence.

True. So position cannot tell the halves apart; language embeddings mark them instead.

True or false: LASER's decoder is told the output language by a language ID embedding, not by the encoder.

True. So the sentence vector has no reason to encode the input language.

True or false: InfoXLM is only slightly better than XLM-R within a language but much better across languages.

True. The explicit crosslingual objectives mainly close the within-across gap.

True or false: In the Transformer encoder-decoder, every decoder layer can attend to all encoder output positions.

True. The source-target context layer does this, removing the RNN’s single-vector bottleneck.

True or false: With a shared vocabulary, an unrelated parent can help an English→Estonian NMT child about as much as a related Finnish parent.

True. Kocmi and Bojar: Czech and Russian parents help as much as Finnish.

True or false: Transferring only a parent's embeddings, without its inner layers, beats training the NMT child from scratch.

False. Embeddings alone are worse than scratch on average; the inner layers carry the benefit (Aji et al.).

True or false: In BART's text infilling, every removed token is replaced by its own [MASK].

False. A whole span is replaced by a single [MASK]; that is what separates it from token masking.

False. With 10K pairs (En-Gu) BLEU stays near zero; pretraining cannot rescue almost no data.

False. Most zero-shot directions improve, but not all (Fr→Zh got worse).

A and C. (B) false: no multilingual model beats the bilingual baselines on the high-resource group (interference). (D) false: low-resource gains are far larger into English.

Multiple selection: which are true of mBART? (A) It is pretrained on parallel data. (B) Its pretraining objective has no crosslingual signal. (C) A language ID token starts the decoder input. (D) Translation fine-tuning requires a new randomly initialised source encoder.

B and C. (A) false: it is pretrained on concatenated monolingual data. (D) false: that is how English-only BART is used; mBART is fine-tuned as is.

Multiple selection: which are true of parent-child transfer learning for low-resource NMT? (A) The child model is initialised with the parent's parameters. (B) It works best when parent and child share the target language. (C) Unfreezing the target embeddings during child training improves Uzbek→English. (D) Transfer from a smaller parent to a larger child works as well as the reverse.

A and B. (B) is Monz’s stated condition; caveat: Aji et al.’s En→De parent transfers about as well to X→English children. (C) false: unfreezing them lowers BLEU, because they overfit the small child data. (D) false: reversed transfer helps little or hurts; it must flow from the larger corpus to the smaller.

Multiple selection: which are true of XLM and InfoXLM pretraining? (A) XLM keeps BERT's next sentence prediction. (B) XLM's TLM is always combined with MLM or CLM. (C) InfoXLM's XLCo classifies whether a sentence pair is parallel from [CLS] representations. (D) InfoXLM is trained from scratch.

B and C. (A) false: XLM drops NSP. (D) false: InfoXLM is initialised from XLM-R.
