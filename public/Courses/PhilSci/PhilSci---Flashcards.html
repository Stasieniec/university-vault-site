L01 Introduction and Logical Empiricism

Flashcards

What does empiricism hold is the fundamental source of substantive knowledge?

Experience and sense perception.

What is the difference between the knowledge rationalism seeks and the knowledge empiricism settles for?

Rationalism seeks certainty and necessity, in at least some domains; empiricism takes knowledge to be normally contingent and fallible.

Why must epistemological and metaphysical positions be kept apart?

They cut across each other: empiricism is compatible with idealism, nominalism with realism about physical objects.

How did Kant combine rationalism and empiricism?

Empiricism about the content of knowledge, rationalism about its a priori conditions.

For Kant, when is a judgment synthetic?

When it extends knowledge instead of unpacking what a concept already contains.

For Kant, when is a judgment a priori?

When its justification does not derive from particular experiences.

What status did the logical empiricists give to logic?

Tautological or analytic: true by form and rules, adding no factual content about the world.

What was the Vienna Circle's regulative ideal of legitimate knowledge?

Knowledge is either empirically grounded or logically/mathematically necessary.

How did philosophy of science's focus shift from the first to the second half of the 20th century?

From the justification of scientific knowledge (a normative ideal) to how science actually works and what scientists do.

What is the difference between syntax and semantics?

Syntax: formal rules for building and transforming expressions, abstracting from meaning. Semantics: the study of meaning.

Which two kinds of statement are meaningful for early Carnap?

Analytic statements (logic, mathematics) and empirically verifiable statements.

What does the verifiability criterion of meaning say?

A factual statement is meaningful only if it is empirically verifiable, in principle, by observation or experiment.

Which principle is summed up as "the meaning of a statement is its method of verification"?

The verifiability criterion of meaning (verificationism).

How does the verifiability criterion solve the demarcation problem?

Unverifiable statements are pseudo-statements, so theories built from them are pseudo-science.

Does verificationism call metaphysical statements false or meaningless?

Literally meaningless: they seem to say something but express no truth-evaluable proposition.

What is philosophy's task for early Carnap?

Logical or linguistic analysis: separating meaningful discourse from grammatical but meaningless pseudo-discourse.

Why did early Carnap work syntactically?

He treated meanings as a matter for psychology or empirical science; formal semantics (Tarski) came only later.

What is the problem with Carnap defining word meaning via a sentence's entailments, truth conditions and verification conditions?

It runs together deduction, truth, verification and meaning, which are not obviously the same; connecting them needs strong assumptions.

In Carnap's arthropod example, what does "x is an arthropod" mean?

x is an animal, x has a segmented body, x has jointed legs: a set of observation sentences.

What does Carnap's arthropod example show?

The reductionist aspiration: a term is meaningful when sentences containing it reduce to observation sentences.

What is a "type confusion" for Carnap?

A grammatical expression that cannot be translated into logically correct form, e.g. predicates applied to “being” or “existence”.

Why is Heidegger's "The Nothing itself noths" a pseudo-statement for Carnap?

It treats “nothing” as a name and as a verb; in modern logic it is neither.

How does modern logic express "nothing is P"?

¬∃xP(x): no object called Nothing is posited.

How does Carnap analyse Descartes's "I think"?

As “there is something that thinks”, ∃xP(x), so no substantial ego is needed.

What did Carnap give up after meeting Gödel and Tarski in the 1930s?

Strict verificationism: he accepted a full semantics for theoretical terms.

What did Carnap's partial interpretation of theoretical terms replace?

The demand for complete reduction to observations, moving him closer to a realist view of science.

Why did Carnap's rejection of metaphysics lose force after his move to semantics?

A full semantics cannot avoid ontology, so the line between theory and metaphysics blurs.

What is a scientific theory on the syntactic conception?

A collection of sentences in a formal logical language.

What are the three components of a theory on the syntactic conception?

Axioms, derived sentences, correspondence rules.

How do theoretical terms like "electron" get meaning on the syntactic conception?

Only through correspondence rules tying them to observational vocabulary.

What is the syntactic conception of theories also called?

The Received View of theories.

Is the syntactic conception a description of laboratory practice?

No, a normative ideal: a rigorous standard for evaluating scientific knowledge.

In kinetic theory, what is the correspondence rule for pressure?

Pressure is the mean force with which molecules strike the container walls.

In kinetic theory, what is the correspondence rule for temperature?

Absolute temperature is proportional to the mean molecular kinetic energy.

How does kinetic theory reach the ideal gas law PV=NkT?

Derive pressure times volume from the molecular axioms in terms of mean kinetic energy, then substitute the temperature correspondence rule.

What is a scientific theory on the semantic conception?

A class of mathematical models representing possible systems.

What three criticisms motivate the semantic conception?

Many theories are not axiomatized; one theory has several equivalent formulations; scientists actually work with models.

Why does language dependence count against the syntactic conception?

One theory has several equivalent formulations, so identifying it with one formulation is wrong.

What is the difference between an iconic and a mathematical model?

Iconic: a concrete representation similar to its target. Mathematical: a formal structure in which a theory’s sentences are true.

Which sense of "model" does the semantic conception mainly use?

The mathematical or logical sense.

How does the semantic conception represent the microstate of a gas of N molecules?

As a point in phase space with 6N coordinates: three position and three momentum per molecule.

What is the difference between how syntactic and semantic conceptions connect theory to the world?

Syntactic: a vocabulary distinction and correspondence rules. Semantic: models linked to target systems by conditions of application.

What does Halvorson argue about the semantic view?

The semantic view, if plausible, is syntactic.

Why does Frigg think neither conception of theories wins outright?

Syntactic is too strict if it allows only formal sentences (science needs models, natural language); semantic also needs a language to specify structures.

Does the syntactic/semantic distinction survive, on the recent debate?

Arguably not: the two collapse into each other or are complementary; Lutz finds no significant difference.

L01b Popper and Lakatos

Flashcards

What is a demarcation criterion?

A necessary condition a discipline must satisfy to count as genuine science; failing it makes the discipline non-scientific.

Why can truth not be Popper's criterion of scientific status?

Science often errs, and pseudoscience may stumble on the truth, so truth and scientific status come apart.

Why does astrology show, for Popper, that empirical evidence cannot be what marks science?

Astrology has a stupendous mass of observational evidence: its method is pseudo-empirical, appealing to observation without meeting scientific standards.

Why did Popper see the explanatory power of Marxism, Freud and Adler as a weakness?

They seemed to explain practically everything in their fields, so confirmations were everywhere and therefore worthless.

What is the point of Popper's example of one man drowning a child and another dying to save one?

Freud (repression, sublimation) and Adler (inferiority feelings) each explain both opposite behaviours, so neither theory takes any risk.

What did Einstein's theory predict for stars seen near the sun during the 1919 eclipse?

They appear shifted away from the sun by a precisely predicted angle, because light is attracted by heavy bodies.

Why did Popper find Eddington's eclipse test of Einstein impressive?

The risk: had the predicted shift been absent, the theory would simply have been refuted.

When should a confirmation count, according to Popper?

Only when it results from a risky prediction: without the theory, we would have expected an event incompatible with it.

Why is it true for Popper that "the more a theory forbids, the better it is"?

Forbidding more gives it more ways to be wrong, so it is riskier and more testable.

Is irrefutability a virtue of a theory, for Popper?

No, a vice: a theory not refutable by any conceivable event is non-scientific.

What is Popper's criterion of demarcation?

The criterion of the scientific status of a theory is its falsifiability, or refutability, or testability.

What does Popper call evidence from a serious but unsuccessful attempt to falsify a theory?

Corroborating evidence.

Why is "well corroborated, therefore probably true" a mistake on Popper's view?

Corroboration only reports past survival of refutation attempts; Popper holds every theory’s probability is zero, and the inference is the inductive step he denies.

Why can no number of observed white swans verify "all swans are white"?

No finite conjunction of instances deductively entails a universal statement (Hume’s problem of induction).

How can one observation falsify a universal law?

By modus tollens: from T→O and ¬O, infer ¬T.

How did astrologers escape falsification, according to Popper?

By making prophecies vague enough to explain anything, which destroyed the testability of their theory.

What is the difference between how Marxism and psychoanalysis fail Popper's criterion?

Marxism was testable and in fact falsified, then rescued; psychoanalysis was never testable at all.

How did Marxists rescue their theory of history from refutation, according to Popper?

They re-interpreted both theory and evidence to agree, a conventionalist twist that made it irrefutable.

Is Popper's verdict on Freud and Adler about the content or the form of their claims?

The form: they may describe facts and contain important suggestions, but not in a testable form.

What does "ad hoc" mean for a modification that saves a theory from refutation?

For this purpose only: its only motive is to absorb the refuting evidence, adding no new testable consequences.

What does Popper call rescuing a refuted theory with an ad hoc auxiliary assumption or re-interpretation?

A conventionalist twist, or conventionalist stratagem.

What does an ad hoc rescue cost a theory, according to Popper?

It escapes refutation only by destroying, or at least lowering, its scientific status. The move itself is always available.

When may a Popperian blame the measurement instead of the theory?

Only with independent reasons for rejecting the measurement, beyond the fact that the theory would otherwise be refuted.

Does Popper hold that any negative result must kill the theory?

No: a prediction never comes from the theory alone, so blame may in principle fall on an auxiliary assumption or the measurement.

What is the difference between what Carnap's verifiability and Popper's falsifiability demarcate?

Verifiability separates meaningful from meaningless; falsifiability separates science from non-science.

Does Popper think non-scientific theories are meaningless?

No: they can be meaningful and important, and myths can develop into testable science.

Why does Lakatos say demarcation is not armchair philosophy?

It has grave ethical and political implications: the Church banned Copernicus and the Soviet Party condemned Mendelian genetics as pseudoscience.

How does Lakatos formulate Popper's criterion of demarcation?

Scientific if one will specify in advance a potential falsifier; pseudoscientific if one refuses.

What does Popper's criterion really demarcate, according to Lakatos?

Scientific method from non-scientific method, rather than theories: status depends on the holders’ attitude.

What is Lakatos's main objection to Popper's falsificationism?

It ignores the remarkable tenacity of theories: scientists have thick skins and do not drop a theory because facts contradict it.

What do scientists do when facts contradict their theory, according to Lakatos?

Invent a rescue hypothesis for a “mere anomaly”, or, failing that, ignore it and work on other problems.

Why does Popper's test fail to separate Newton from Marx, according to Lakatos?

Some Newtonians, asked to name a falsifier, would be as nonplussed as some Marxists.

Why does Lakatos refuse Kuhn's conclusion that theory change is irrational?

Then there is no demarcation, no distinction between progress and decay, and no objective standard of honesty.

What is Lakatos's unit of appraisal?

The research programme: a series of theories over time sharing a common structure.

Why do "progressive" and "degenerating" apply to a Lakatosian research programme and not to a single hypothesis?

Those words describe a direction of travel over time, and a single snapshot has none.

What are the four components of a Lakatosian research programme?

Hard core, protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, positive heuristic, negative heuristic.

What is the hard core of the Newtonian research programme?

The three laws of mechanics plus the law of gravitation.

Why is the hard core of a research programme irrefutable, for Lakatos?

By methodological decision of its practitioners, not by logic or meaning.

What does Lakatos call the part of a research programme that absorbs anomalies?

The protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses.

What does Lakatos's protective belt contain?

Auxiliary hypotheses, initial conditions, theories of the instruments, models of the system.

What does Lakatos call the rule never to direct modus tollens at the hard core?

The negative heuristic.

What is Lakatos's positive heuristic?

The problem-solving machinery: a partial plan for which models to build next and which anomalies to digest.

How does a Newtonian handle a planet that does not move as predicted, according to Lakatos?

Changes the protective belt: checks atmospheric refraction or light in magnetic storms, or invents an unknown planet. Gravitation is never on trial.

Why can "still unrefuted" not mark science, for Lakatos?

All theories are born refuted: every programme has unsolved anomalies at every stage.

Which "refutations" of Newton and Einstein does Lakatos cite?

Lunar motion against Newton, Kaufmann’s results against relativity. Curd & Cover note both were later shown spurious.

When is a research programme progressive, for Lakatos?

When it predicts novel facts, undreamt of or contradicted by previous or rival programmes: theory runs ahead of the facts.

When is a research programme degenerating, for Lakatos?

When its theories are fabricated only to accommodate known facts: theory lags behind the facts.

What kind of question does Lakatos's criterion make of scientific status?

A historical question about track record, which can change: programmes can recover or run out.

What novel fact does Lakatos treat as the flagship of the Newtonian programme?

Halley’s comet: from a brief stretch of its path, Halley predicted its return in seventy-two years, and it returned.

What novel fact did the Einsteinian programme predict, according to Lakatos?

Two stars’ separation measured during an eclipse would differ from their separation measured at night.

Which predictions of Marxism failed, according to Lakatos?

Absolute impoverishment of workers; first revolution in the most industrial society; no revolutions under socialism; no conflict between socialist countries.

How did Marxists explain rising working-class living standards, according to Lakatos?

With a theory of imperialism, cooked up after the event.

Why are refutations not the hallmark of empirical failure, for Lakatos?

All programmes grow in a permanent ocean of anomalies.

What counts as the hallmark of empirical progress, for Lakatos?

Dramatic, unexpected, stunning predictions; a few are enough to tilt the balance. Trivial verifications count for nothing.

What is the difference between how Popper and Lakatos judge a modification that saves a theory?

Popper judges its motive and form; Lakatos judges its consequences: did it lead to novel facts?

Is a protective-belt modification automatically ad hoc, for Lakatos?

No: it is normal, constant and necessary; only the absence of later novel predictions damns it.

Why does Lakatos judge an invented planet and the Marxist theory of imperialism differently?

Same manoeuvre, but one can lead to a novel discovery and the other produced nothing.

How do scientific revolutions happen, according to Lakatos?

Scientists desert a degenerating programme for a progressive rival.

What does Lakatos mean by saying his methodology offers no instant rationality?

Budding programmes deserve leniency, may take decades to progress, and no rule says when to give up.

Is it dishonest to stick with a degenerating research programme, for Lakatos?

No, provided the record is kept public.

What does Lakatos mean by "there is no refutation without a better theory"?

A programme is removed by a better rival, not by a Popperian quick kill.

What does Lakatos say Popperian crucial experiments and Kuhnian revolutions turn out to be?

Myths: what normally happens is that progressive programmes replace degenerating ones.

How does Lakatos contrast Kuhn's account of scientific revolutions with his own?

Kuhn, as Lakatos reads him: a sudden, irrational change in vision. Lakatos: rational desertion of a degenerating programme for a progressive one.

What is the difference between Popper's and Lakatos's attitude to scientists' tenacity?

Popper: a vice that lowers scientific status. Lakatos: rational and often correct, since all theories are born refuted.

What is the cost of Lakatos's position?

Verdicts are only retrospective: you cannot tell a scientist today he must abandon a programme.

L02 Kuhn on Scientific Practice

Flashcards

What question does Kuhn put in place of logical empiricism's focus on the justification of scientific knowledge?

How does science actually work: what do scientists actually do, in real historical context?

What does Kuhn mean by saying observation is theory-laden?

Observation is partly shaped by theoretical commitments, so there is no fully theory-neutral observational language.

What is the difference between the positivist and the Kuhnian periodisation of science's history (Galison)?

Positivist: observation is one continuous band under changing theories. Kuhn: theory and observation are co-periodised, so their breaks line up.

What does Kuhn's co-periodisation of theory and observation imply about theory change?

There is no continuous, theory-neutral observational base: when the paradigm changes, what counts as an observation changes too.

What is a Kuhnian paradigm?

The shared framework through which a scientific community practises its science, and which holds that community together.

Besides theoretical principles, what elements does a Kuhnian paradigm include?

Methods, standards, tools, exemplars, metaphysical assumptions.

What does Kuhn call a concrete, standard problem-solution that students learn to imitate?

An exemplar.

Name an exemplar of the Newtonian paradigm.

The harmonic oscillator (mass on a spring), or the Earth-Sun system.

What is the difference between a Kuhnian paradigm and a theory?

A paradigm is wider: besides theory it includes methods, tools, standards, exemplars and metaphysical assumptions.

What is Kuhn's normal science?

Research within an accepted paradigm that solves the paradigm’s puzzles instead of testing whether the paradigm is true.

What is a puzzle in Kuhn's sense?

A problem whose solution is expected in principle under the accepted framework.

In Kuhnian normal science, who is blamed when a puzzle is not solved?

The scientist, not the paradigm: “only a poor workman blames his tools.”

Why does Kuhn think the conservatism of normal science allows progress?

Because the foundations are not constantly reopened, researchers can work on problems in detail.

How does Kuhn's normal science contrast with Popper's ideal of science?

Kuhn: the paradigm is accepted unconditionally, so science is conservative. Popper: theories face permanent critical testing.

What is the difference between Popper's and Kuhn's reasons for denying astrology is a science?

Popper: astrology is unfalsifiable in practice. Kuhn: it lacks a puzzle-solving tradition, so no normal science (standard reading, not settled in course material).

What is a Kuhnian anomaly?

A puzzle that persistently resists solution and appears inconsistent with the paradigm’s expectations.

What distinguishes a Kuhnian anomaly from an ordinary failed experiment?

An ordinary failure is blamed on error or poor technique. An anomaly persists and threatens the paradigm itself.

What is a crisis in Kuhn's model?

Important anomalies undermine confidence in the paradigm, which itself becomes a topic of discussion.

What happens during Kuhn's extraordinary science?

Researchers entertain competing approaches, and even the standards for judging theories become contested.

What is a scientific revolution for Kuhn?

A non-cumulative change in which a new paradigm replaces the old one.

Give Kuhn's cycle of scientific development in order.

Paradigm and normal science, anomaly, crisis, extraordinary science, revolution (then new normal science).

What are Kuhn's three stages in the history of chemistry?

Alchemy (preparadigmatic), phlogiston paradigm (Priestley), oxygen paradigm (Lavoisier).

Why does Kuhn count the shift from phlogiston to oxygen as a revolution?

The framework changed: the relevant substance, explanatory vocabulary and interpretation of weight changes, more than one new fact.

What is the difference between the phlogiston and the oxygen accounts of combustion?

Phlogiston: release of phlogiston from burning bodies. Lavoisier: combination with oxygen.

Why does Kuhn compare a revolution to a Gestalt switch, as regards how the change happens?

A revolution is sudden and holistic, like a conversion, not a piecemeal accumulation of new facts.

Which view of observation does Kuhn's Gestalt-switch comparison express?

Observation is theory-laden: what scientists see depends on the paradigm they bring to it.

Where does Kuhn's Gestalt-switch analogy break down?

The viewer knows the lines stayed the same, but a scientist has no external standpoint to check the world stayed the same.

What does the "My Wife and My Mother-in-Law" priming experiment show about paradigms?

The prior image acts like a paradigm: the same stimulus is seen as a different whole depending on what the viewer brings.

In Bruner and Postman's playing-cards experiment, what did subjects do with anomalous cards at short exposures?

They identified them as normal cards, without any sense of trouble.

What does Kuhn take the playing-cards experiment to show about discovery?

Anomaly is at first not perceived: it is recognised only against the background of expectation, and only with effort.

How can the Müller-Lyer illusion be used against strong theory-ladenness?

It persists even when you know the lines are equal, so perception is partly insulated from belief (Fodor).

Does Kuhn's theory-ladenness mean scientists in different paradigms live in different physical worlds?

No. Observation and interpretation are intertwined, so what an observation shows depends partly on the paradigm.

What is Kuhn's incommensurability?

Two paradigms cannot be fully measured against each other by a common language or fixed set of standards.

Why are the side and diagonal of a unit square incommensurable?

2​ is irrational, so no common unit measures both exactly.

What does the side-and-diagonal analogy imply about comparing incommensurable paradigms?

They lack a common measure but can still be related and compared.

What is semantic incommensurability?

Meanings change across paradigms: “mass” in Newtonian and Einsteinian physics, “planet” across the Copernican shift.

What is methodological incommensurability?

Standards change: what counts as a satisfactory explanation before Newton may differ after Newton.

What problem about progress does incommensurability create?

If meanings and standards change, in what sense is a new paradigm objectively better?

What did Kuhn later soften incommensurability into?

Local untranslatability: some terms cannot be translated without loss of their inferential relations.

Why does the "local" in local untranslatability leave room for comparing paradigms?

The failure is confined to a cluster of interdefined terms (mass, force, energy), and most of the two languages stay intertranslatable.

What are the main criticisms of Kuhn?

Irrational social victory, no demarcation criterion, unclear “paradigm”, relativism (no objective criteria for progress).

What does Lakatos accuse Kuhn of vindicating?

“Mob psychology”, by comparing science with political revolutions.

What are Kuhn's five values of theory choice?

Accuracy, consistency, broad scope, simplicity, fruitfulness.

Which of Kuhn's values requires consequences beyond the observations a theory was designed for?

Broad scope.

Which of Kuhn's values is about opening new research and discoveries?

Fruitfulness.

In what sense are Kuhn's five values objective?

They are shared by the scientific community.

Why do Kuhn's values not work as an algorithm for theory choice?

Each is imprecise and they can conflict, so scientists weigh and rank them differently.

Why does Kuhn think rational disagreement is needed for progress?

If everyone switched at once, competing possibilities would not be developed or tested.

How does Kuhn answer the charge of relativism?

Theory choice rests on shared epistemic values: rational, though not algorithmic.

What is the difference between Weber's value relevance and value freedom?

Value relevance: values may select research questions. Value freedom: they must not be presented as empirical conclusions.

What did the logical positivists hold about value statements?

They express subjective attitudes and cannot be true or false.

In the triptych model of science, which panel must be value-free?

The middle one, scientific research. Research agenda and application may involve values.

What does Heather Douglas call the triptych model of values in science?

The externality model, because values are kept outside the middle panel, scientific research.

What does the weak value-free ideal allow inside scientific research?

Epistemic values only. Non-epistemic values stay in agenda setting and application.

What is the difference between epistemic and non-epistemic values?

Epistemic serve cognitive aims (accuracy, simplicity). Non-epistemic are e.g. justice, political preferences.

What do internality positions (Longino, Douglas) claim against the value-free ideal?

Non-epistemic values can sometimes legitimately play a role inside scientific reasoning.

What is Longino's contextual empiricism?

Data support a hypothesis only relative to background assumptions.

How, according to Longino, do values enter scientific reasoning internally?

Through background assumptions, which shape what is measured, modelled, compared, interpreted or counted as explanation.

For Longino, what secures objectivity if no individual is free of assumptions?

Transformative criticism within a community, which exposes and revises background assumptions.

What are Longino's four conditions for objectively transformative discourse?

Recognised avenues for criticism, shared standards, responsiveness to criticism, equality of intellectual authority.

For Longino, how does a claim become scientific knowledge?

By surviving critical emendation and modification by others, which frees it from any individual’s subjective preferences.

What social mechanisms secure objectivity for Longino?

Replication with variations, peer review, treatment after publication, absorption into the body of knowledge.

What is a direct role for values in Douglas's account?

Values determine a decision or course of action, compatible with the externality picture.

What is inductive risk (Douglas)?

Under uncertainty, the consequences of error affect how much evidence is needed to accept or reject a claim.

Do values become evidence for a hypothesis in Douglas's inductive-risk account?

No. Where false positives and false negatives cost differently, values set the evidential threshold.

What is the difference between Douglas's and Longino's accounts of values in science?

Douglas: values set thresholds under uncertainty. Longino: values enter background assumptions that link data to hypotheses.

L03 Under-determination

Flashcards

For Popper, how does science come to accept its empirical basis?

By intersubjective agreement. No objective facts force it, so accepting a falsifying report is ultimately a human decision.

Duhem's thesis: if premises P1​,…,Pn​ entail O and experiment establishes O′ incompatible with O, what follows?

The conjunction of the premises is false. It does not follow that any particular premise is false.

Which thesis is summed up as "falsification is ambiguous"?

Duhem’s under-determination thesis: modus tollens refutes the conjunction, never a named premise.

What is a crucial experiment ( experimentum crucis)?

“An irrefutable procedure for transforming one of the two hypotheses before us into a demonstrated truth.”

Why, for Duhem, is no crucial experiment possible?

A hypothesis is only ever tested together with auxiliary assumptions, including the theory of the experimental apparatus.

Besides auxiliary assumptions, what else stops a crucial experiment from deciding between two hypotheses, according to Duhem?

Unconceived alternatives: hypotheses nobody has thought of yet, which the experiment cannot rule out (developed later by Stanford).

What is the telescope example of Duhem's thesis?

An astronomical theory conflicts with telescope observations, but nothing says whether the astronomy or the optics of the telescope is at fault.

According to Duhem, what decides which belief to give up when logic does not?

Good sense (bon sens): some opinions no contradiction refutes are still “perfectly unreasonable”.

What is the difference between Duhem's conclusion and saying theory choice is arbitrary?

Duhem says choice is not formal: logic does not decide, but good sense still does.

What does De Haro question about Duhem's good sense?

What good sense actually is, and how a scientist can be “an impartial and faithful judge”.

What are Quine's two dogmas of empiricism?

Reductionism and the analytic-synthetic distinction.

What does the dogma of reductionism claim, according to Quine?

Every scientific statement is equivalent to one in a special empiricist language: the verification theory of meaning.

What is Quine's objection to reductionism?

You cannot test isolated statements: no statement has its own private stock of empirical consequences.

What is Quine's objection to the analytic-synthetic distinction?

The difference between conceptual scheme and fact is “of degree only”.

For Quine, what are analytic statements?

The limiting case of true statements: no empirical content, confirmed however the world turns out.

Why does Quine say the two dogmas are "at bottom identical"?

Both assume statements can be assessed one at a time against experience. Deny that and both fall together.

Where does Quine's web of belief touch experience?

Only along the edges. A conflict at the periphery forces readjustments in the interior.

What sits at the centre of Quine's web of belief?

Mathematics and logic.

Why do logic and mathematics sit at the centre of Quine's web of belief?

Revising them forces the largest rearrangement. They are revisable, just expensive, not a different kind of truth.

How does Quine extend Duhem's under-determination thesis?

Duhem’s bundle of auxiliary assumptions becomes everything you believe, so almost any statement can be kept true by paying elsewhere.

Which claim of Quine's says even a statement near the periphery can be saved by pleading hallucination or amending logical laws?

Any statement can be held true come what may, given drastic enough adjustments elsewhere in the system.

What does Stein's criticism of Quine target?

Quine assumes an empiricist must be sceptical about meanings, dismissing Frege-Carnap intensions instead of engaging them.

What is the difference between sense and reference (Frege, Carnap)?

Sense (intension) is the linguistic meaning of the words. Reference (extension) is the object they pick out.

What does Laudan say discussions of under-determination have proceeded in?

An “evaluative vacuum”: assuming that if a move is logically possible, its rationality need not be asked about.

According to Laudan, what would Quine need to show for "held true come what may" and never does?

That the ampliative rules of theory choice are always too ambiguous to decide between any two theories.

Why, for Laudan, is saving a theory by pleading hallucination not reasonable, even though it is possible?

Rationality includes epistemic warrant, chiefly the empirical evidence. Logically possible is not rational.

What is Laudan's non-uniqueness thesis of under-determination?

For any theory and evidence supporting it, there is at least one rival as well supported.

What is Laudan's egalitarian thesis?

Every theory is as well supported by the evidence as any of its rivals.

Which of Laudan's two under-determination theses is a threat to epistemology and methodology?

Only the egalitarian thesis.

Why does the non-uniqueness thesis of under-determination not threaten methodology?

A rival existing does not make it equally good in every respect. Methodology’s job is choosing among the survivors.

Why would the egalitarian thesis make methodology idle?

It says there is never anything to choose on, so theory choice would be arbitrary.

What does Quine's empirical under-determination (1975) require of two theory formulations?

Empirically equivalent (same observation sentences) but theoretically inequivalent (different theoretical sentences).

Why is empirical under-determination a problem for scientific realism?

If the data cannot decide between rivals, which one are you a realist about?

What is the difference between semantic and epistemic under-determination?

Semantic concerns meaning and interpretation, independent of the evidence. Epistemic concerns what the evidence can settle.

Dualities are potential cases of which kind of under-determination?

Flashcards

In Hempel's deductive-nomological model, what is an explanation?

A deductive argument in which the explanandum is deduced from general laws plus particular conditions.

In Hempel's model, what is the explanandum?

The phenomenon (or law) to be explained: the X in “why X?“.

What are Hempel's four conditions of adequacy for a D-N explanation?

Explanandum is a logical consequence of the explanans; a general law used essentially; empirical content; explanans true.

What does Hempel claim about the relation between explanation and prediction?

They are symmetric: same logical structure, differing only in whether the conclusion is already known.

What is inductive-statistical (I-S) explanation?

The covering law variant with a probabilistic law: the explanans shows the explanandum was highly probable.

What does the paresis case show against inductive-statistical explanation?

Untreated syphilis explains paresis though only a minority of cases develop it: high probability is not necessary for explanation.

Must the laws in a Hempelian D-N explanation be causal?

No. Functional laws count: PV=nRT explains without saying which quantity causes which.

Is Hempel's D-N model meant to describe how scientists actually explain?

No, it is normative: the ideal of a good explanation, like the logician’s concept of proof.

What does the asymmetry objection to Hempel's D-N model say?

Deductions can run both ways, explanations cannot, so the D-N model admits backwards explanations.

In the flagpole case against Hempel, which direction of deduction explains?

Pole height to shadow length explains; shadow length to pole height only predicts.

Why does the flagpole case show the D-N conditions are not sufficient for explanation?

The shadow-to-pole deduction meets all four conditions of adequacy yet explains nothing.

How did Hempel reply to the asymmetry objection?

He bit the bullet: if his theory allows both directions, both are fine. Hopeless for the flagpole.

What does the hexed-salt example show against Hempel's D-N model?

Irrelevance: true, used premises (the hex) can satisfy the D-N conditions yet explain nothing.

Why does a falling barometer predict a storm without explaining it?

Both are effects of a common cause, the drop in atmospheric pressure. Correlation is not explanation.

What does "the window broke because the ball hit it" show against Hempel's D-N model?

Explanations need not involve laws: citing a causally relevant fact can be enough.

What do the flagpole, hexed-salt and barometer counterexamples to Hempel have in common?

All are missing causation: the pole causes the shadow, the hex causes nothing, barometer and storm share a cause.

What is explanation according to Salmon's causal theory?

Uncovering causes: situating the explanandum in a nexus of causal relations, the mechanisms that produce it.

How does a causal theory of explanation solve the flagpole problem?

Sunlight hitting the pole causes the shadow, not the reverse, so explanation runs pole to shadow.

What price does a causal theory of explanation pay?

It must say what causality is, which empiricists since Hume have regarded as suspect.

What is causation on the regularity theory?

Constant conjunction: C regularly followed by E, producing an expectation in us, not a necessary connection.

What is causation on the counterfactual theory?

If cause C hadn’t occurred, effect E wouldn’t have occurred (Woodward).

Which theory of causation, held by Salmon, says we can identify causal processes and interactions in nature?

The process theory (picture a chain of falling dominoes).

Why does the regularity theory of causation bring back the barometer problem?

Barometer drops are constantly conjoined with storms, so they would count as causes.

What is Hume's problem as it applies to causal theories of explanation?

How to distinguish causation from correlation.

Why might causal theories of explanation be too narrow?

Explanations in quantum theory, or of a law by a more general law, are hard to phrase causally.

On the unificationist theory, how are phenomena explained?

By fitting them into a broader pattern, reducing the number of facts accepted as brute.

Which two philosophers developed the unificationist theory of explanation?

Michael Friedman and Philip Kitcher.

What is the main advantage of unification over causal theories of explanation?

It accounts for non-causal explanations, such as those in quantum theory.

How does Kitcher handle the flagpole asymmetry?

Causal talk summarises deeper unification asymmetries: deriving shadows from poles fits a far wider pattern.

What is Godfrey-Smith's contextualism about explanation?

Standards of good explanation partially depend on the scientific context: each field and period sets its own.

What is the difference between pluralism and Godfrey-Smith's contextualism about explanation?

Pluralism allows a fixed short list of relations; contextualism lets each field and period set its standards.

What is Kuhn's Newtonian gravity example of changing standards of explanation?

Newton gave a law and no mechanism; it became accepted that the right mathematical law counts as explanation.

Why is Godfrey-Smith's contextualism about explanation not "anything goes"?

A conception of explanation can embed a factual error, e.g. requiring citation of God’s will when there is no God.

For van Fraassen, does explanation add to our beliefs about how theory relates to the world?

No. It is a reason to accept a theory for purposes, so not an epistemic aim of science.

What kind of relation is explanation for van Fraassen?

A three-term relation between theory, fact and context; an explanation is an answer.

What is the difference between van Fraassen and Godfrey-Smith on explanation?

Both make it contextual: van Fraassen puts it external to science, Godfrey-Smith internal to scientific reasoning.

Why does van Fraassen's constructive empiricism need explanation to be non-epistemic?

Otherwise inference to the best explanation would push him to believe in unobservables.

What is Hempel's eliminativism about understanding?

Understanding is epistemically irrelevant: a topic for psychology, not philosophy.

What is reductivism about understanding?

Explanation is understanding enough: no separate theory of understanding is needed.

What separates Khalifa's reductivism about understanding from Hempel's eliminativism?

Khalifa reduces understanding to knowledge and explanation but still finds it philosophically interesting.

What is Lipton's view of understanding?

Not super-knowledge but simply more knowledge: knowledge of causes.

Why does Trout think the feeling of understanding is no epistemic guide?

The Eureka! feeling results from cognitive biases such as overconfidence and hindsight.

What are Carnap's three senses of "understanding"?

Pragmatic (capability of use), subjective (intuitive feeling), epistemic (knowing semantic rules).

Subjective (varies with individual psychology) versus useable for certain aims (compatible with objectivity).

It only works if pragmatic means subjective; read as useable for aims, understanding can be epistemic.

Explanation, requirements of adequacy (consistency, empirical confirmation), useability.

Reductivists accept only explanation and adequacy; the others add useability.

An ability to act: it involves skills and judgement.

Macro (science as a whole), meso (scientific communities), micro (individual scientists).

Hempel’s explanation is two-place (T, P); De Regt’s understanding is at least four-place (S, C, P, T).

Once new conceptual systems are settled, they too become anschaulich: visualizability is not a fixed standard.

It was valued for increasing intelligibility but is not necessary for understanding: one tool, valued in some contexts.

L05 Scientific Realism and its Critiques

Flashcards

What does the ontological question of realism ask?

Whether the external world exists independently of us, of our thoughts and perceptions.

How does Berkeley's idealism answer the ontological question of realism?

No: the world is mind-dependent. Esse est percipi, to be is to be perceived.

Why is Berkeley's idealism called "the maximally empiricist possible answer"?

Our only access to anything beyond ourselves is perception, so nothing beyond perception can ever be checked.

Which question does Kant move to, away from the ontological question of realism?

The epistemological question: what knowledge of the external world can we have?

What is the difference between Kant's noumena and phenomena?

Noumena are things-in-themselves, real but unknowable. Phenomena are appearances, structured by our mind, and knowable.

What question do Eddington's two tables raise for the realism debate?

Which table is real: the solid everyday table or the mostly empty scientific one, or both, and how they relate.

What status does Mach give to claims about unobservable reality?

They are metaphysical and cannot have a truth-value, since they cannot be checked by direct observation.

What is the view that theories are economical descriptions of sensory experience, tools for prediction rather than descriptions of hidden reality?

Instrumentalism, held by Mach.

How were the early logical positivists more extreme than Mach?

Mach tied truth to observability. Carnap and Neurath tied meaning to it as well: unverifiable claims are meaningless.

What were the three commitments of the early logical positivists on theory and observation?

Verifiability theory of meaning, a sharp theory-observation distinction, theoretical sentences introduced through observational ones.

Why did the logical positivists need the theory-observation distinction to be sharp?

They tied meaning to observability, and meaning does not come in degrees: nothing is “almost meaningful”.

Which two changes moved the later logical empiricists towards realism?

Acceptance of semantics (theoretical sentences can be true or false), and verification replaced by confirmation.

How does Grover Maxwell's argument use glasses and microscopes against the theory-observation distinction?

Glasses count as observation, a microscope is the same in principle (more lenses), and there is no principled cut-off on the continuum.

What does scientific realism claim?

Empirically well-confirmed theories are true or approximately true, including what they say about unobservables.

What is the difference between scientific realism and empirical adequacy?

Empirical adequacy needs only the observable claims true. Realism also needs the unobservable parts (approximately) true.

What are the three theses of scientific realism?

Semantic (about language), epistemic (about knowledge), metaphysical (about what there is).

How do the three theses of scientific realism combine into realism and anti-realism?

A realist accepts the conjunction of all three. An anti-realist rejects at least one.

What does semantic realism claim?

Read theories literally: “there are electrons” means there is a thing with the properties the theory ascribes.

What does epistemic realism claim?

We are justified in accepting well-confirmed theories as true, including what they say about unobservables.

What does metaphysical realism claim as a component of scientific realism?

The theory is (approximately) true: the world is as it says, and its posited entities exist.

What is the difference between semantic and metaphysical realism about electrons?

Semantic: claims about electrons are literally true or false. Metaphysical: electrons actually exist.

What are the two senses of "metaphysical realism" in the realism debate?

An external world exists independently of the mind (against Berkeley). A theory is approximately true and its entities exist (the contested one).

Which theses of scientific realism does van Fraassen accept?

Only semantic realism. He does not accept epistemic or metaphysical realism about unobservables: agnostic about electrons, not denying them.

How does Putnam state the no-miracles argument?

“Realism is the only philosophy that does not make the success of science a miracle.”

Which examples illustrate the no-miracles argument?

The Higgs boson and the top quark: unobservables predicted on theoretical grounds before any experiment.

Why can empirical adequacy alone not explain novel predictive success, according to the no-miracles argument?

Fitting past observations does not explain getting right observations nobody has made yet.

In the no-miracles argument read as an IBE, what is the best explanation of what?

Approximate truth of theories best explains their success predicting experiments never performed before.

What is inference to the best explanation also called?

Abduction.

How does IBE differ from deduction?

IBE is ampliative and fallible. Deduction is neither: it is truth-preserving and only takes out what is in the premises.

How does IBE differ from induction?

Both are ampliative and fallible. Induction appeals to observed frequencies, IBE to explanatory considerations.

What makes an inference ampliative?

Its conclusion goes beyond what is logically contained in the premises.

Why does IBE fail to move many anti-realists, such as van Fraassen?

They do not accept explanation as an aim of science, so “best explanation” gives no reason to believe.

How does van Fraassen state constructive empiricism?

“Science aims to give us theories which are empirically adequate; and acceptance of a theory involves as belief only that it is empirically adequate.”

In which two respects do van Fraassen's formulations of realism and constructive empiricism differ?

The aim of science (literal truth or empirical adequacy) and what acceptance commits you to believing (truth or only empirical adequacy).

What is the disagreement between van Fraassen and the scientific realist about?

Unobservable entities only. They agree about observables.

Does van Fraassen say electrons do not exist?

No. He is agnostic: the evidence does not oblige belief, and believing in unobservables is “up to us”.

What rival hypothesis does van Fraassen offer to the realist's account of IBE?

We believe the theory that best explains the evidence is empirically adequate, which fits our practice equally well.

What does van Fraassen call the no-miracles argument, using Putnam's name for it?

The Ultimate Argument.

What are van Fraassen's three moves against Maxwell's objection?

Take theories literally, the distinction is a category mistake, “observable” is vague but usable.

Which two distinctions does van Fraassen say the theory-observation distinction runs together?

Theoretical versus non-theoretical terms (language) and observable versus unobservable entities (objects).

Why does a vague observable/unobservable line refute the logical positivist but not van Fraassen?

The positivist hung meaning, all-or-nothing, on it. Van Fraassen hangs only epistemic commitment on it, where clear cases suffice.

What historical premise does Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction start from?

Many theories now considered false were once empirically successful.

Which terms are the standard examples of non-referring central terms in Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction?

“Aether” and “phlogiston”.

What does Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction conclude?

We are not justified in believing our best theories true, or that their posited entities are real.

Why is Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction a meta-induction?

The induction runs over theories, not over observations.

What assumption about our epistemic situation does the pessimistic meta-induction rely on?

We have no reason to think it differs from that of past scientists, who were just as sure and turned out wrong.

What is convergent realism?

Theories are approximately true, and successive theories get closer and closer to the truth.

What are the three elements of convergent realism that Laudan attacks?

Approximate truth, reference preserved across theory change, empirical success of old theories explained by new ones.

What are Laudan's three problems for convergent realism?

No good definition of approximate truth, no justification for the success-reference link, central terms of once-successful theories do not refer.

What do Laudan's three problems for convergent realism have in common?

They all stem from approximate truth, load-bearing for the realist and extremely hard to make sense of.

Why is approximate truth hard to make sense of, even formally?

Truth, like meaning, seems not to come in degrees: something is true or false.

Through which route does the realist link empirical success to reference?

Success → approximate truth → reference.

Why does Laudan deny that reference and empirical success go together?

Referring theories can be unsuccessful (old tectonic plate theory), and successful theories can fail to refer (aether).

How do aether and phlogiston theories show that success does not indicate approximate truth?

Their central terms do not refer, so they cannot be approximately true, yet they were successful.

An induction on the past: past successful theories were false, so current ones are likely false too.

What is the second of Saatsi's two readings of the pessimistic meta-induction?

A timeless argument against the no-miracles argument: success is not a reliable indicator of truth.

Why is Saatsi's second reading of the pessimistic meta-induction harder to resist than the first?

It need not predict today’s theories are false. Past successful-but-false theories alone break the success-to-truth inference.

What, according to Musgrave, do ultimate explanations do?

They remove puzzlement.

According to Musgrave, are non-ultimate explanations real explanations?

Yes, although they relocate and enhance puzzlement instead of removing it.

What does Musgrave say about the demand for explanation in science?

Theories do aim to explain, and better or deeper explanations are always possible, but there is no unlimited demand for explanation.

What does Musgrave accuse van Fraassen of conflating?

Realism with essentialism, and the demand for explanation with the demand for ultimate explanation.

Constructive empiricism (van Fraassen).

Their approximate or probable truth, not their literal truth.

“Approximately true” can mean the important parts are true even if others are false, checkable part by part.

The maturity requirement is arbitrary and post hoc, and some listed theories were well confirmed: phlogiston (combustion) and caloric (heat).

Caloric theory and nineteenth-century optical aether theories were mature and successful yet are considered false.

Mathematical structure (the equations), even when the posited entities are dropped.

The electromagnetic field, empty space, or nothing at all.

Even if the terms refer, the theories were radically misguided, so cannot be approximately true.

Realism needs approximate truth, and reference is only a precondition for it.

Idle constituents.

Explicate reference and approximate truth together, and do not project current insights into the past.

Why does De Haro deny that convergence on a causal core is "virtually guaranteed"?

Eliminating idle terms could leave none of the original terms, so a surviving causal core is highly remarkable.

What is De Haro's second criticism of Stanford's critique of selective realism?

Stanford reads into Maxwell’s text that transmission without an aether was “incoherent” and “unintelligible”.

What weakness does De Haro find in the realism literature from Putnam to Stanford?

It assumes a single kind of meaning of words, which produces Laudan’s perplexity about reference.

Which premise of the pessimistic meta-induction does De Haro's claim about Laudan's list attack?

That old theories contain obviously non-referring terms. De Haro: not at all obvious.

What is the intension (Frege's sense) of a word?

Its linguistic meaning.

What is the extension (Frege's reference) of a word?

The actual object or entity the word refers to.

What does the "morning star" / "evening star" example illustrate?

Two different senses, one reference: both names pick out Venus.

How do the thermodynamic and statistical-mechanical definitions of temperature differ?

Thermodynamics: tendency of an object to give energy to its surroundings. Statistical mechanics: mean kinetic energy of the molecules.

On De Haro's account, what do the thermodynamic and statistical-mechanical uses of "temperature" share?

The same extension (one physical quantity), despite different intensions.

How does the intension/extension distinction reinterpret Kuhn's claim that scientists in different paradigms "live in different worlds"?

The intension changes between theories, the extension need not: both still refer to and measure the same thing.

What are the two steps of De Haro's answer to the pessimistic meta-induction?

  1. Continuity of extensions of successive theories (extensional equivalence). 2. Define approximate truth in terms of extensions.

What is extensional equivalence between two theories, for De Haro?

Reference to the same items in the domain of application.

What is De Haro's standard example of two extensionally equivalent theories?

Quantum and classical mechanics: all extensional results of classical mechanics derive from quantum mechanics.

How does De Haro's extensional equivalence secure continuity differently from Psillos' selective confirmation?

Psillos: via theory parts (retained core). De Haro: by delimiting the extension to a domain of application.

What is the slogan of De Haro's extensional scientific realism?

We are justified in being realists about extensions but not necessarily about intensions.

Why should a realist commit only to a theory's extension in its domain of application, according to De Haro?

The intension covers untested situations where the theory may be false; evidence supports only the extension where it was tested.

What determines the extension of a term in a scientific theory, according to De Haro?

The intension plus the specific circumstances or context in which the phenomenon is studied.

What four aspects determine extensions in natural science, according to De Haro?

Model specification, free parameter values, extra-theoretical facts, approximations and idealisations.

What three kinds of correspondence establish extensional equivalence, for De Haro?

Predictive (numerical and formal), material, conceptual.

What is conceptual correspondence between two theories, for De Haro?

The concepts are not identical but match: they play the same roles in the given extension.

Quantum mechanics' expectation values obey the form of Newton's F=−∇V. Which kind of De Haro correspondence is this?

Predictive (numerical and formal) correspondence.

The quantum theorist and the inorganic chemist study the same atom with different experiments. Which kind of De Haro correspondence is this?

Material correspondence.

"Position of a particle" plays the same role in quantum and classical mechanics. Which kind of De Haro correspondence is this?

Conceptual correspondence.

In the pessimistic meta-inductivist's case as De Haro presents it, which kind of correspondence is missing between aether-based and modern electromagnetic theory?

Conceptual; predictive and material correspondence hold.

According to De Haro, what is Maxwell's "aether" extensionally equivalent to?

The electromagnetic field together with a fixed frame of reference, v≪c.

What was Maxwell's argument for the existence of the aether, which De Haro calls very good?

Conservation of energy: energy cannot vanish at the sender and reappear at the receiver, so something carries it.

According to De Haro, are "aether" and "electromagnetic field" the same in intension?

No: intensionally distinct, extensionally equivalent in the domain.

How does De Haro's extensional realism escape Stanford's "Pyrrhic victory" dilemma?

It distinguishes no theory parts: assuming sound arguments, all terms scientists claim refer do refer.

How does De Haro's extensional realism answer Stanford's anachronism objection about Maxwell?

It agrees with Maxwell that the aether was essential and real; what his argument established is, extensionally, the field.

What do De Haro's extensional realism and van Fraassen's constructive empiricism have in common?

Both read theories literally but restrict belief: van Fraassen to the observable, De Haro to extensions.

Why is De Haro's extensional realism still a realism, unlike constructive empiricism?

Extensions include unobservables (fields, electrons); the observable/unobservable line plays no role.

What is the difference between Psillos' selective realism and De Haro's extensional realism?

Psillos believes some parts of a theory; De Haro believes the whole theory, but only extensionally in its domain.

What is the difference between how Kitcher and De Haro save the reference of old terms like "aether"?

Kitcher saves it token by token; De Haro saves it homogeneously for every well-motivated term.

In De Haro's sketch, when is theory T closer to the truth than T′?

Iff T is extensionally true in a larger, and more significant, domain of application.

Whose idea of scientific progress does De Haro use to define approximate truth?

Laudan’s: progress measured by the relative number and significance of the problems a theory solves.

In De Haro's account, what stops a theory counting as closer to the truth merely by becoming more general and vaguer?

It must also be as good as a theory as its rival, e.g. in predictive power and explanation.

On De Haro's account, is an old theory refuted when a successor is extensionally true on a larger domain?

No: it remains extensionally true, so approximately true, on its own domain.

What does De Haro say Stanford's "repeated, profound, and unpredictable changes" in theory miss?

Restricted domains of application where successive theories are extensionally equivalent.

What is the difference between verificationism and Popper's falsifiability as criteria?

Verificationism is a criterion of meaning; falsifiability is a criterion of demarcation between science and non-science.

For Popper, is an unfalsifiable statement meaningless?

No. It can be perfectly meaningful, just not scientific. Metaphysics is meaningful and sometimes fruitful.

Why does Carnap say metaphysical statements say nothing?

They have no method of verification, so by the verifiability criterion they are pseudo-statements that only look like assertions.

What is the standard objection to the verifiability criterion of meaning?

It is self-undermining: the criterion is neither analytic nor empirically verifiable itself.

Name the internal challenges to early logical positivism.

  • Stating the verification criterion precisely
  • The problem of theoretical terms
  • Quine’s attack on the analytic-synthetic distinction

Name the external challenges to early logical positivism.

Popper replacing verifiability with falsifiability, and later Kuhn’s historical turn.

How did later logical empiricism soften early logical positivism?

  • Verification gave way to weaker confirmation
  • Reduction of theoretical terms to observation relaxed
  • Syntactic conception of theories gave way to the semantic one

Why does Kuhn hold that cumulative acquisition of novelty is improbable in principle?

Novelty is what the paradigm does not anticipate, so it can only appear as an anomaly, which counts as a failure rather than a contribution.

For Kuhn, how does genuine novelty enter science?

By rupture (revolution) rather than by accumulation.

Why are scientific revolutions necessary for Kuhn?

Normal science cannot correct its own foundations; only a paradigm change restores exhausted puzzle-solving capacity.

Why are arguments in paradigm debates necessarily circular for Kuhn?

Paradigms supply the standards for judging arguments, so each side argues from criteria the other does not accept.

To what does Kuhn compare the choice between competing paradigms?

The choice between competing political institutions: a choice between incompatible modes of community life.

Why are successive paradigms incommensurable for Kuhn?

They differ in problems, standards and meanings of terms, so there is no neutral vocabulary for a fair comparison.

What is the standard pushback against Kuhn's incommensurability?

It is often overstated: scientists do communicate across paradigms and cite empirical successes both sides recognise.

Why does Lakatos call Popper's falsificationism naive?

Scientists do not in fact abandon a theory the moment it meets a contrary fact.

Why does Lakatos say Kuhn makes science irrational?

On Kuhn, paradigm change is a change of commitment with no rational criterion, so no demarcation is possible.

How does Lakatos nuance Popper?

He appraises a series of theories over time instead of one theory against one test, leaving room for scientists’ tenacity.

How does Lakatos modify Kuhn?

He keeps the historical unit but restores a rational criterion for choosing between research programmes.

What is the case for Lakatosian scientific value being an objective property of a theory?

Progressiveness is judged against the public record of predictions and corroborations, independent of anyone’s opinion.

What is the case against Lakatosian scientific value being objective?

Lakatos gives no principled cut-off for when to call a programme degenerating, so the verdict comes only with hindsight.

For Longino, where does the objectivity of science come from?

From science being a social enterprise: objectivity is a property of a community’s practice.

For Longino, what produces scientific knowledge?

The community subjecting individual work to criticism. Knowledge is what survives transformative interrogation.

Does objectivity differ in kind for non-empirical sciences, on Longino's view?

No, against the logical empiricists.

Is Longino's contextual empiricism an ethical theory?

No. It is a theory of scientific objectivity and epistemic methodology; it does not say whether research is permissible.

Which theories decide whether a piece of research is ethically permissible?

Consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics, care ethics, theories of justice.

How is Longino indirectly relevant to research ethics?

She encourages scrutiny of the assumptions built into research and of whose perspectives are included in criticism.

What is the difference between Laudan's non-uniqueness and egalitarian theses?

Non-uniqueness: some rival is as well supported as a theory. Egalitarian: every theory is as well supported as any rival.

Which of Laudan's underdetermination theses would make theory choice arbitrary?

Only the egalitarian thesis, and Laudan argues nothing in Quine establishes it.

What is the difference between a theory formulation and a theory for Quine?

A formulation is a sentence, in practice the conjunction of the axioms; the theory is what the formulation expresses.

For Quine, when do two theory formulations express the same theory?

When they are empirically equivalent and one converts into the other by reconstrual of predicates.

What is Quine's example of two formulations that count as the same theory?

Two physical theories that differ only by exchanging the words “molecule” and “electron”.

Which empirically equivalent formulations does Quine's underdetermination need?

Ones that no reconstrual of predicates can bring into agreement, so they are different theories.

For Quine, in which direction does implication run between theory and data?

Theory plus standing conditions implies observation conditionals; the data never imply the theory.

Why do single sentences of a theory usually have no observation consequences of their own, for Quine?

Holism: observation consequences follow from the theory as a whole.

Why does Quine think holism lends credence to underdetermination?

A recalcitrant observation can be absorbed by revising many different sentences, so more than one system seems to fit all data.

According to Laudan, what kind of claim does holism establish?

One about logical possibility: any hypothesis can be retained against any evidence by adjusting something else.

According to Laudan, what kind of claim is the underdetermination that threatens methodology?

One about rational warrant: the evidence supports rivals equally (the egalitarian thesis).

According to Laudan, what would Quine need to show to get from holism to the egalitarian thesis?

That the ampliative rules of theory choice are too weak to prefer one repair over another.

What is Laudan's phrase for Quine arguing without ever examining the rules of theory choice?

Arguing in an “evaluative vacuum”.

According to Laudan, which underdetermination thesis does holism support at most?

Non-uniqueness: some rival exists.

Why is the non-uniqueness thesis harmless, according to Laudan?

Choosing among rivals is exactly what methodology is for.

What is inference from data to a hypothesis about the hidden structure that would explain it called?

Explanatory inference.

How does the causal account of explanation handle the flagpole case?

Sunlight and pole cause the shadow, so the pole’s height explains the shadow and the reverse derivation does not.

What challenges face the causal account of explanation?

  • It needs an account of causation, suspect to empiricists since Hume
  • It seems too narrow for explanations hard to state causally

What must the unification account of explanation recover without appealing to causes?

The flagpole asymmetry.

How does Kitcher try to recover the flagpole asymmetry?

He argues causal talk is a loose summary of deeper unification asymmetries.

Which concessions between causal and unification theorists support pluralism about explanation?

Salmon accepted unification and Kitcher accepted causation.

How does Godfrey-Smith's contextualism about explanation go beyond pluralism?

There is no fixed list of explanatory relations: each field sets its own standards.

What is the case against Godfrey-Smith's contextualism about explanation?

It is unclear what makes the field-specific forms all explanation, and it risks not saying what makes an explanation good.

According to De Regt and Dieks, what unifies the plural forms of explanation?

Understanding, as their common aim.

Is explanation internal or external to science for van Fraassen?

External: an application of science to answer our questions, adding nothing to what we believe about the world.

Who treats explanation as a three-term relation between theory, fact and context?

Van Fraassen.

Van Fraassen and Godfrey-Smith both call explanation context-dependent. What do they actually disagree about?

Whether explanation is epistemic: Godfrey-Smith makes it internal to scientific reasoning, van Fraassen external.

What is the relation between explanation and understanding for De Regt and Dieks?

Understanding requires an explanation plus the skill to construct and use explanations.

Why can understanding, as a skill, unify causal, unificatory and law-based explanation?

Each is a way a skilled scientist can come to understand a phenomenon.

Which two senses of "pragmatic" do De Regt and Dieks separate?

Subjective, and useable for certain aims.

In which sense is understanding pragmatic for De Regt and Dieks?

Useable for certain aims, which is compatible with epistemic relevance.

Why does Hempel count understanding as epistemically irrelevant?

He treats “pragmatic” as “subjective”.

What does Trout reduce the sense of understanding to?

Cognitive bias.

Why is understanding epistemically relevant for De Regt and Dieks?

It is a macro-level aim of science, and it is needed to produce the explanations and models that yield knowledge.

For De Regt and Dieks, what is the condition for understanding a theory?

Its intelligibility: scientists can use it, as measured by CIT.

If not subjective, what does CIT make scientific understanding?

Contextual: relative to a community and its level of progress.

Which historical case makes CIT's contextual intelligibility look subjective?

Schrödinger versus Heisenberg: two groups in one discipline disagreeing about what was intelligible.

What is the defence of CIT against the Schrödinger-Heisenberg dispute?

The disagreement was settled by the community over time, as Pauli predicted.

Why do conceptual toolkits for qualitative reasoning operate at the meso-level?

They are taught and shared within a discipline; individual scientists have them to different degrees.

What is the case for understanding as an additional epistemic aim of science?

It explains the historical variation (Newton, Huygens, Maxwell; Schrödinger versus Heisenberg) that a single fixed standard of explanation cannot.

How does De Regt and Dieks's account of understanding as a skill answer Trout?

A skill that produces empirically adequate models is not a feeling.

What is the case against understanding as an additional epistemic aim of science?

The skill may be a means to the real epistemic aims (truth, empirical adequacy) rather than an aim itself.

How could a reductivist like Khalifa reply to understanding as an additional epistemic aim of science?

Call the skill knowledge plus competence, so nothing needs adding to the list of aims.
